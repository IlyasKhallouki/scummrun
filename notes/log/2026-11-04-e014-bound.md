# 2026-11-04: E014, a bound on the reading and scopes grown as needed, on the interactive demo

The interactive Fate of Atlantis demo had stalled on one thing: from the state after its opening dialog, a reading of
the 8-room scope ran past 50 minutes. Each try from there waited on it. Two general changes went in, each behind its
own switch and each switched on for Monkey Island too:

- **(a) a bound on the try's reading:** 300 s, set from the readings of both games. Past the bound the try goes on
  without the model: the interface's inputs, the waits and the delays.
- **(b) scopes that start with the player's room** and grow only when a target, a plan or the model-free step needs a
  neighbouring room.

Plan and amendments (4) and (5): `experiments/E014-fate-demo/plan.md`. The cloud VM could not start (the zone had no
capacity), so the run was on the laptop: two workers, seed 1.

## The result

The explorer stopped by its stagnation rule after 37 tries, in 89 minutes, without reaching the game's end. Its 46
entries all lie in room 49, the room the game opens in, and all 45 past the root replay from boot with 0 differences.

What it did, in the game's own numbers:

- It booted to the first decision point at frame 992 and waited through the opening to frame 1042, where the verbs come
  on.
- From there it tried the interface's inputs: each of the ten verbs, and clicks in the room that set six flags and a
  handful of variables. One click turned on a dialog verb.
- No object changed owner, state or class, and no other room was entered. The furthest entry is 214 frames past the
  first decision point.

## Where the time went

| class | share of the try wall |
|---|---:|
| reading bound | 57% |
| interface, no yield | 36% |
| productive | 5% |
| overhead | 2.5% |

Every model try from a state after the opening, 18 of 18, reached the bound. So the planner never saw any of those
states, and the run became a model-free run in room 49. Its model-free steps found the verbs and the flags in the
first nine tries, and nothing new in the 28 tries after.

## What the measures say about the reading

- **The room count is not the cost.** From the state after the opening, the reading of room 49 alone took 1,256 s on
  the laptop, against more than 50 minutes for the 8-room ring. The growth rule worked as written: room 49's scope
  started as {49} and took room 42 when a guide atom needed it. But the 2-room reading from these states still did not
  end within the bound. The cost sits in a few of the reader's event runs that take minutes each, at states where the
  game's interface is live.
- **A wall bound is only as good as the stop behind it.** The in-process reader looks at its stop flag only while it
  waits for its workers, and a worker ends only when its run ends.
  - So the stopped readings kept running after their tries had moved on. By the 26th try, 14 reading threads and 8
    sets of workers were running: a load near 20 on 8 hardware threads, for the rest of the run.
  - The first version also left a stopped reading's idle workers waiting forever on a poisoned lock. That was fixed
    before the run.
  - The clean form of the bound is a reading in a child process that the bound can kill. It is recorded, not applied.
- **The reader already had a watchdog.** It stops a reading after 60 minutes of wall. The earlier note that a reading
  had no wall bound was not exact. Without the new bound, a try from these states would wait an hour and then fail.

## On Monkey Island, with both switches on

- B3's 15 tries came out the same as E013's run with every switch on. 10 of their readings ran on the bound's thread,
  and none came near 300 s. Their 126 new entries replay from boot.
- The kitchen's goal chain replays from boot.
- A 4-try smoke run from the boot read room 33 alone in every try and replays from boot.

So both changes leave Monkey Island's checks as they were, and neither moved the demo past its opening room.

## Two breaks met during the run, recorded and not applied

- **The stopped readings' load,** as above.
- **One free step gave the engine an input that the bridge refuses.** The error ended that whole try, where it should
  have been one refused trial. It reproduces from the same entry as a plain free try, so it belongs to E013's free step,
  not to the bound.

## What this leaves

The demo's progress past room 49 needs the model at the states after the opening, or a model-free step that changes an
object. In two hours of the interface's inputs, two levels deep, no object changed. The reader's cost at those states
is the open question: what in its event runs takes minutes when the interface is live, and whether that cost is the
game's or the reader's.
