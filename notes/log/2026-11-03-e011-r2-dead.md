# 2026-11-03: E011, the no-plan proofs merged into round 2

The kitchen check stopped at 65 tries. In it, targets with no plan each cost the planner its whole limit. One of them
was bit:118 = 1 from the two entries near frame 2770. The dead-target branch answered that with two switches:

- sound no-plan proofs (h^1, then h^2) before the search;
- deferral of a timed-out target with a doubled limit.

Round 2 had separately made the planner faster with the Rust port of Fast Downward's lama-first-costs and fast forms
of the per-start reductions. This note is their merge: the gates again, and what the merged build does to E009's 15
kitchen tries and to the check's bit:118 tries. Plan and amendments: `experiments/E011-speed/plan.md`; numbers:
`experiments/E011-speed/results/round2-dead/`. Seed 1 only.

## The merge

The branches touched the same planner function and the same request record, and nothing else in common. The proofs now
run on the reduced task before every search arm, the Rust lama-first-costs included. With both switches off, the
merged build gives the old results byte for byte (G7). It gives the old explorer on fake steps (G9) and the reference
route on test (a) with Fast Downward's binary (G10).

## Exact with everything on

With every round-2 switch and both new switches on:

- **Test (a).** It reaches the kitchen on E007's line in strict, ready and ready-with-the-old-tail modes, and its 8 new
  entries verify from boot.
- **The 15 tries.** Under both the reference and the fast settings they equal the Python reference's in every stop,
  frame, input and entry.
- **The recorded requests.** All 41 rebuildable ones come back as recorded:
  - 40 return their recorded plans step for step;
  - one, bit:118 = 1, is proven dead;
  - the states of each found plan lie inside the h^2 rows;
  - the fast h^2 equals the plain one.

## What the proofs cost and what they save

The 15 tries have no dead target, so the proofs can only cost there.

- **Per request.** They cost 0.25-0.90 s per ring(33) request that reaches the search, and under 0.01 s per ring(38)
  request: 3.2 s over the 15 tries.
- **The median try.** 4.3 s with everything on, against 4.5 s for round 2 alone and 4.8 s for round 1, alternating one
  by one on the same load.
- **The other 14 tries** sum to 70.4 s against 68.7 s.
- **The first try** (a fresh ring(33) reading and two refinements) was 18 s longer in the merged sequence, 14.6 s of it
  in the reading. The reader binary is the same in both. The same try varied from 100 s to 297 s between the stopped
  starts, so one sample of it does not separate the binaries.

On the check's own bit:118 tries the gain is the whole limit:

- **Merged, everything on:** each try ends in 12-25 s with "no plan (the planner proved none)". h^2 takes 5.9 s of a
  19 s request, because the goal fact never enters the pair fixpoint.
- **Round 1 and round 2 alone:** 190-204 s each, ending on the 180 s limit. Round 2's Rust search does not end there
  either.

## The search was not what took the time

The dead-target branch set the deepening's first limit L0 to 120 s, the p90 of the found plans' search time. It
measured that with Fast Downward's binary, where the check's requests on the refinement reading searched for 89-120 s.

The same requests, replayed with the Rust lama-first-costs, take 0.07-0.53 s of search and 1.5-1.8 s of landmark graph
and relaxed task. The port repeats Fast Downward's search (the same plans here, and the same counts on the route
component's 30 requests), so Fast Downward's 89-120 s were mostly not its expansions. They were the rest of its run on a
155 k-operator task, at load 3-15. On a smaller ring(33) task the planner profile put Fast Downward's text read at 7.1 s
and its landmark graph at 16.1 s. How the 89-120 s divide was not measured.

On the Rust path the amendment's own rule gives L0 = 10 s. A dead target that h^2 does not prove still costs the whole
limit with either search: on bit:118, neither search ends within 180 s.

## What binds

Unchanged by the merge: the reading is most of a try, and the median try is a fresh ring(38) reading of about 4 s. What
the merge adds is that a target h^2 proves dead costs seconds instead of the planner's limit. Targets that are dead
without an h^2 proof still cost the limit and wait for the deepening.
