# 2026-11-01: E003 plan step A0, fork exactness under load

Question: is a child forked at the kitchen start the same run as a fresh engine given the same inputs, frame by frame,
while 7 other children run? Lemma and premises: `experiments/E003-path-through-cloud/fork-exact.md`. Bench:
`fork_exact.py`. Numbers: `results/fork-exact.log`, `results/fork-exact-dump-check.log`.

Prediction (the plan, before the run): 0 differences. An earlier fork bench at the same start measured 5.8 ms median
per fork (21 ms max), and 490 child frames per second at load 12 and 860 at load 7.

Answer: 0 differences. Over 3,000 frames from frame 235, the child C and its parent P equal the fresh run F at every
frame: 6,002 comparisons of stop status and full dump. The 1,506 load children equal F at all 6,402 chunk ends. P's
trace equals F's. The span has 22 room changes, 357 frames with a cutscene open and 13 CD track starts.

## The dump switch

The state dump had neither the RNG state nor the bridge clock. The stop status has both. The new bridge switch
`SPEEDRUN_DUMP_EXACT` (EngineConfig.dump_exact, off by default) adds an `exact` object to the dump:

- the RNG state;
- the quarter-frame clock and the segment start;
- the raw input cursor, and the mouse and buttons;
- the audio pump's counters;
- the CD music timer and its phase, the CD status and `pollCD`;
- the speech timer;
- the running sounds and the sound and MIDI queues;
- the camera;
- a 64-bit digest of the four virtual screens and the palette.

The build is `/var/tmp/fast-mi/svm-window` (`--no-reset`, sha1 87066f13be73).

Lemma D's check: three engines in lockstep from boot to frame 3,235, with every other switch off. They are the shared
fr1-main build, the new build with the switch off and the new build with it on. 3,234 frames:

- the stop status differs on 0;
- the dump with the switch off differs from the old build's on 0;
- the dump with the switch on, without `exact`, differs on 0;
- the three trace.jsonl files are equal (sha1 3c4fdad27400).

## The run

Laptop, nice 10, seed 1, run B's recorded inputs from boot with run B's settings. Every engine dumps with the locals
and the exact object.

1. F boots and steps frame by frame from 235 to 3,235, keeping each status and dump.
2. P boots to 235 and forks C, 7 load children and a control child.
3. Each load child runs in 100-frame chunks and is compared with F at each chunk's end. A child that reaches the end
   is replaced by P's next fork.
4. C and P step in lockstep and are compared with F on every frame. P forks once per frame, as A4's parent will.

The first run had no control child. Its result file was replaced by the committed run's, so its numbers are only
here. Child frames per second count the load children's run commands only, not their dumps. The load includes this
bench's 9 engines and other jobs: Rust builds, a reading, a planner and a test runner.

## The control

The control is a child of P at frame 235, with its RNG state's low bit flipped (`set_rng`). With no load, it differs
from F at frame 236 in the status, in the dump without `exact` (var 100) and in `exact`. It then differs on all 3,000
frames. So the comparison sees a one-word change at once.

## What this shows and leaves open

1. The window search's premise holds on this span: a child forked at a frame plays on exactly as a fresh run, RNG and
   clock included, and so does the parent after 3,967 forks. That includes `closeRoom` at every fork, with 22 room
   loads after it (lemma F1).
2. The comparison is a projection. It does not see walk internals of undrawn actors, iMuse's internal state beyond
   sound status, or arrays and strings that no var holds yet. C5's merging needs a save-stream digest and its own
   lemma.
3. Fork time and child speed depend on the load. At load 21-30 a fork took 17-26 ms median, 3-4 times the earlier
   bench's 5.8 ms at load 12. A child ran 146-296 frames per second, against 490 at load 12. A4's cost estimates used
   5.8 ms and 490.
4. G1 holds here because the music driver is the emulated AdLib (boot record music_type 8). Hardware MIDI and MT-32
   emulation install timer threads, which a child does not have, so K must keep pinning the driver.
