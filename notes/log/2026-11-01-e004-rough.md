Question: if every job is read only to N steps, with each path the limit ends made a havoc record, does the Part I
reading finish, and does lama-first find a plan from it to Part I's end? Proof:
`experiments/E004-fast-exact/rough-reading.md`. Numbers: `experiments/E004-fast-exact/results/rough-*`.

## Prediction, before the runs

- The five havoc_cut holes the p1r check found are closed at cd700e9 (fixes da0e003, 48dd7f8, 049cf14), so none needs

## Why small N saturates: closures that name every unit

At N = 100 on Part I without further changes, 155 of the first 21,566 havoc records named all 573 units of the scope
(2,116 effects and 14,072 events each), and their events made pass 1's event round 2 24,847 runs, against 508 in the
full reading. READER_TRACE's havoc lines now say why a closure names every unit (e52a091). The causes were starts
whose operand does not fold: `startObject` by a local in the inventory hook (global 9), the sentence script (global 2)
and global 33, `startScript` by a variable in globals 2 and 47, and two that remain:

- global 23 (started from room 85's exit through global 17, and from globals 1 and 106): `startScript(Var[396 +
  Local[0]])`, where Local[0] comes from the cursor position;
- global 73 (started from global 114 and global 116): `startScript(Local[1])`.

The switch havoc_starts (havoc-cut.md, 5c3511b) names, for an object start that does not fold, each object's entry for
the verb (or its 0xFF entry), and for a start by a variable its values. It does not reach the two above, and a room
change puts every scope room's exit code in the closure, so a closure that can change the room reaches room 85's exit,
global 17 and global 23, and names every unit (576 units with the starts it adds).

Where the cuts fall:

- **N = 100.** 170,421 of the 170,589 havoc records stand in room 58's entry code (one unit each, 70 effects, 359
  events: the event of the slot "entry" at every wait point of every room's entry code). The 155 that name every unit
  come from round 1, through global 23 and global 73. Their events make round 2 24,847 runs.
- **N = 300.** Every havoc record names every unit (576 units). They all come from the five
  `dialog_line(120..124)[202@85]` jobs, whose paths stand in room 85's local 202 > global 114 > global 73 (> global 17
  > global 23) at the cut. With reach_pairs, which drops that family, the same happens in round 5 in the sentence jobs
  on two inventory objects (500 havoc records per job).
- **N = 500 and 1,000.** No path of the slow jobs passes the limit. Rounds 4 to 7 of the second N = 1,000 run took
  217, 742, 1,893 and 892 s; with reach_pairs, which drops the `[202@85]` dialog family, round 4 took 2.6 s and
  rounds 5 to 7 526, 1,643 and 1,208 s. The only havoc records are the `dialog_line(·)[204@28]` jobs' (the var:164
  loop, 9 per job, closures of 0 to 6 units), as at the machine's own limit, and rounds 1 to 6 write the same records
  at both. The time goes where it went in the full reading: the
  slowest jobs at N = 1,000 were `event(script:114@101)` (1,498 s, and 942 s with its list), the dialog jobs at
  `[202@85]` (150 to 274 s each) and the sentence script's events (58 to 106 s).

So the step limit does not give a fast reading on Part I: the slow jobs are slow from forks and merges, not from long
paths (the dialog jobs' paths are 300 to 500 steps long, the global 114 event's under 1,000), and a limit short enough
to cut them cuts them where their continuation reaches a room change, and from there global 23, which names every
unit. Such a record has 2,141 effects and 14,072
events with unknown locals, and the reader holds them per record: 49 to 56 GB within minutes.

## The Part I scope's rooms

The Part I scope (`reader-scope --part1`, the rooms of results/groundtruth-part1.jsonl's moves) has 31 rooms.
`rooms_from_files.py` follows the room changes the scripts make, from seed 1's start (room 33), with no ground truth:
a constant `loadRoom`/`loadRoomWithEgo` room, every constant a variable used there is assigned, a global's argument
used as the room (each constant start's argument), and `actorFollowCamera` to each constant room a `putActorInRoom` of
the same unit gives. That reaches 80 rooms, all 31 of the scope among them, and 49 more
(results/rough-rooms-from-files.json). With constant rooms only it reaches 66, 30 of the 31: room 42 is entered
through a global script's argument. So the game files alone find every room of the scope, and more than twice as many; they do not say which belong to Part I.

## Plan to Part I's end

No Part I reading finished, so lama-first had no Part I task, there is no Part I plan, and nothing to compare with
results/groundtruth-part1.jsonl's key goals. So no N was found where the plan stops being trivial: at N = 100 and 300
the reading does not finish (memory, every-unit havoc records), at N = 500 and 1,000 it costs what the full reading
costs and had not finished pass 1 after 4,875 s and 22,554 CPU s.

What would change this, from the measurements: a havoc closure that does not name every unit when it reaches global
23 or global 73 (their operands are a local and an index on a local, so this needs the locals' values at the start,
not only constants), and room changes that name only the rooms the closure can load and leave, not every scope room.
Without both, a cut in any of the slow jobs is a record that can do anything.
