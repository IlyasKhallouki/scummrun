# 2026-11-03: E012, the kitchen reached from the boot

E011's kitchen check explored from the boot for 65 tries and never reached the goal (owner:566 = 1, the k = 2 scope's
10 rooms). E012 added a layer that splits each step by who decides it: the player's moves are planned, the game's
moves (time passing, random draws) are played by the engine, the interface's own inputs are tried where the model
cannot act, and the game's end marks a move dead. This note is the rerun of that check with every switch of the layer
on. Plan and amendments: `experiments/E012-game-mechanics/plan.md`. Numbers: `results/kitchen/`. Seed 1 only, laptop,
two workers.

## The result

The explorer reached the goal at its 53rd try, 21.5 minutes after it started from the boot, with no step by hand. No
other stop rule fired first.

The goal entry's movie replays from boot with 0 differences on all 32 of its checkpoints, on two engines: the lean
build through the Rust verify, and the reference build through the Python reference's verify. It holds 85 inputs over
15,688 frames. All 150 entries of the archive replay from boot with 0 differences.

## How the goal came

The research for this plan had found that E011's one request for the goal came from an entry idle in a dialog, not
from one waiting on the cook. The rerun shows it end to end.

- The explorer reaches room 28 through rooms 35, 29 and 33.
- From the first room-28 entry, which has control, the goal plans. Its walk to the cook's door only works once the
  door is open. The window search's play when ready gives up after 294 tries, 96 frames before the door opens at the
  paused script's due frame, and the window runs empty.
- The next room-28 entries are in a dialog, which the planner's reading cannot model (Lemma R's R0 on the dialog
  variable). From them, the no-plan proof (h^2) shows the goal unreachable in 2 to 8 seconds. It shows the same from
  each frame where waiting changed a tested fact. E011 had spent 3 minutes per target at the planner's limit on such
  entries, 24 minutes in one try.
- The affordance step clicks the dialog's lines. One returns control.
- From that state the goal plans and plays in 10 seconds. Its walk to the cook's door ends exactly when the door opens:
  273 frames later, the due frame of the paused script, where the wait had stopped. This time the due frame is inside
  play when ready's 294 tries, and the window search finds it by forking 253 times in 2.3 seconds.

So the two things the plan set out to handle were both on the path: a dialog the planner cannot see through, and a
timer the engine plays out. The timer was found only because its due frame happened to fall inside the window
search's bound; the due-frame window, not built, would give it directly.

## What the layer did

- **The interface's inputs** carried three of the chain's eight steps. 42 affordance steps tried 5,767 inputs and kept
  33 new states. Every stop on R0 and every empty window was followed by one.
  - In dialogs a step takes about half a second.
  - In a sentence idle it takes 30 to 130 seconds. Each of its 260 to 550 trials runs the wait afterwards, frame by
    frame. These steps are 44% of the tries' wall.
- **The wait** ran at all 53 entries, with a median of 0.09 s. From three of the four room-28 entries it stopped on
  the cook's door exactly at the paused slot's due frame.
- **The goal again** from the wait's stops ran 6 times. Each was proven dead in seconds.
- **Targets per key** kept var:103 from taking the tries over: it was never a try's last target (E011: 29 of 65).
- **The delay fork** triggered twice, on draws whose range the scripts do not give, so it changed nothing.
- **No move** ended the game.

## The chain is long

The chain is 15,688 frames, against 488 for the best known route. 14,400 of them come from four windows of E010's
search that ran to their 3,600-frame bound without the planned input taking effect. The parent idles to that bound,
and the next entry continues from there. The due-frame window, which would give a timer's event its exact frame
instead, is not built.
