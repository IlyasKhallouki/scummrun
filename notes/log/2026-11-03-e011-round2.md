# 2026-11-03: E011 round 2, the second integration

Question (E011's): can one try of the explorer take well under a second, and a route replay milliseconds, on this
laptop, exactly, with everything that runs written in Rust? Round 1 left the answer at "partly": a ring(33) try at
17.5 s, a median try at 3.8 s, and the Rust search's route failing test (a). Round 2 sent six builders at what bound:
the engine's two output cuts, play's round trips, the reader in the process, the planner's per-start reductions, Fast
Downward's lama-first-costs in Rust, and the try's own time. This note is their integration: the merge, the gates with
every round-2 switch off and on, and the benchmark against round 1's binaries. Plan and amendments:
`experiments/E011-speed/plan.md`; numbers: `experiments/E011-speed/results/round2/`. Seed 1 only.

Answer after round 2: still partly, now with every gate passing. One resident process runs E009's 15 kitchen tries at
the same median as round 1 (4.9 s; a fresh ring(38) reading, which no round-2 item touched), the ring(33) tries in a
median of 6.1 s instead of 17.6 s, and the 15 tries in 193 s instead of 265 s, measured interleaved on the same load.

## The integration

The six branches merged without a conflict between them. The builders' reports were lost, so what went in is what
they committed:

- **engine**: patch 0003, a mixer that only counts samples and no pixel output (SPEEDRUN_CUTS), each argued first in
  CUTS.md;
- **play**: patch 0004 (an observed frame, a run with its dump and a batch of hit tests, each one message; the screen
  layout in the status), and the window search using them, with the hit cache keyed by the layout;
- **read**: the reading as a library module, the memo's store in memory, and --prep, where the workers do the reading
  thread's and the sink's share of their own records. The library was not yet wired into fmi;
- **plan**: a fast form of the per-start reductions and of the SAS build, h_FF by watched preconditions, work on
  threads, each with a check mode that runs the reference beside it and compares;
- **route**: Fast Downward's lama-first-costs in Rust, down to libstdc++'s hash-table iteration order, because the
  landmark code's output depends on it;
- **own**: the idle rule on each frame's changes, the player's teardown off the try's path, parses done once.

The integration added the own builder's interface method next to play's (the only conflicts, both sides kept), turned
--prep and the watched h_FF for the lama search into switches of the integrated binary, and built the engine with
all four patches into its own folder.

## The gates

Everything that was exact in round 1 is exact with the round-2 switches on, and with them off:

- fork exactness over 3,000 frames with 33,260 load children (all six engine switches) and 9,758 (the four lean ones),
  0 differences;
- the door and kitchen chains in every switch setting, against svm-w3b, 0 differences; with no pixels the dump is
  compared without its pixels and on a pixel-free digest;
- every new message of patch 0004 against the commands it stands for, with and without the cuts, 0 differences;
- the reader byte for byte with --prep at 3 and 6 threads, and the memo on with and without it;
- the planner's fast forms in every check mode: the reductions' task, the build, every h_FF evaluation and the
  successor order equal the reference's on every request, and the PlanResults equal the Python reference's;
- the Rust lama-first-costs equal to Fast Downward's (landmark graph, plan file, counts) on 13 fixed requests;
- test (a) reaching the kitchen on E007's line in strict, ready and ready-with-the-old-tail modes with the Rust
  lama-first-costs, entries verified from boot;
- the 15 tries equal to the Python reference's in every stop, frame, input and entry, with the switches off, on, and
  in the fast settings; 129 new entries verified from boot.

So G10, round 1's one failing gate, passes: the fast settings now play the reference's routes, because they run the
reference's search. Round 1's lesson stands behind it: a valid plan of the reading's task is not always playable, and
the search that picks which valid plan matters to the route.

## The benchmark

Two things changed how it had to be run. The job memo writes nothing while its disk is under 8 GB plus twice its
largest file, and / sat between 6 and 9 GB free while other experiments ran; in a first start the memo stored in round
1's first try and not in round 2's, ten minutes later. The benchmark was stopped and run again with the memo off in
both sequences (an amendment), so it compares the code and not the disk's level. And the lock that serializes heavy
steps was shared with two other runs, so each try's wall excludes its wait for the lock, and the two binaries' tries
alternate so that both meet the same load.

| | median of 15 | ring(33) | ring(38) | sum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| before (Python, round 1's B4) | 29.4 s | 186.2 s | 23.8 s | 1,780 s |
| round 1's binaries, fast settings (Gbfs) | 4.9 s | 17.6 s | 1.75 s | 265 s |
| round 2's binaries, fast settings (Rust lama-first-costs) | 4.9 s | 6.1 s | 1.3 s | 193 s |
| round 1's binaries, reference settings (Fast Downward) | 6.3 s | 43.5 s | 2.75 s | 425 s |
| round 2's binaries, reference settings | 4.8 s | 5.9 s | 1.25 s | 199 s |

Where the time went:

- **The planner** gained the most. On the plan component's fixed ring(33) sequence a request for another start takes
  3.0-3.2 s (7.0-7.6 s with round 1's Gbfs, 20-23 s with Fast Downward's binary), another goal 4.4 s. The per-start
  reductions fell from 5-10 s to 2.0-3.4 s and are now two thirds to three quarters of a request. The Rust
  lama-first-costs' own work is 0.7 s (the landmark graph, the relaxed task, the successor trie, 0.1 s of search),
  where Fast Downward spent 13-18 s; on test (a)'s first request the watched h_FF evaluates in 2.95 ms where round 1's
  took 23-34 ms.
- **Play**: test (a)'s third window in ready mode takes 4.5 s (17.1 s with the round-2 switches off), under its 5 s
  target. In the benchmark play is under 1% of a try.
- **The try's own time** fell from 80-113 ms to 13-14 ms, the idle measurement from 0.2 s to 0.1 s, and the engine's
  replay of the door chain from boot to 0.12 s with both cuts.
- **The reading** did not get faster. A fresh ring(33) reading takes 36-41 s with round 2's reader and 35-36 s with
  round 1's; --prep moves 10-15% of the reader's CPU off its own threads without shortening the wall, and the reader
  holds 200-250 MB more. The library form that would keep the reader and its memo inside the process is unfinished.

## What binds now

The reading is 74% of round 2's fast sum: the first try's fresh ring(33) reading and two refinements (106 of 193 s), a
refinement from another entry (21 s, which the memo cut to about 10 s in round 1), and three fresh ring(38) readings of
4.4-4.8 s that set the median. The planner is the other 25%, mostly the per-start reductions. Everything else is under
2%. Round 2 gained 27% of the fast sum (53% of the reference sum), so by the stop rule a third round goes to the
reading: the reader inside the process with its memo in memory, and the readings' own wall at 3 threads.

## A process detail found on the way

The round-1 G12 watcher counts every process named fmi as a root. During this benchmark it flagged `python3 -c` inside
the kitchen check's `fmi explore run`: that run starts each job under guard.sh, whose last line computes its wall time
with python3. No python ran in the benchmark's own processes; the watcher was rooted at them for this round. The
kitchen check's process tree, as the explorer runs it, holds that python3 for a few milliseconds per job.
