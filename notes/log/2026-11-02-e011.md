# 2026-11-02: E011 round 1, everything that runs in Rust, integrated

Question: can one try of the explorer (return to an entry, read the rooms around it, plan, play) take well under a
second, and a route replay milliseconds, on this laptop, exactly, with everything that runs written in Rust? Plan,
interfaces, formats and predictions, written before any code: `experiments/E011-speed/plan.md`. Six components were
built in parallel against fixed interfaces (engine, play, readio, readcore, plan, explore), each gated alone. This note
is the first integration: the merge, the gates on the integrated build, and the benchmark. Numbers:
`experiments/E011-speed/results/`. Seed 1 only.

Answer after round 1: partly. A route replays in under a millisecond by fork and in 0.33-0.43 s from boot. One
resident process runs E009's 15 kitchen tries in a median of 3.8 s per try (29.4 s before, measured on the same load),
1.1 s on ring(38) and 17.5 s on ring(33). Every exactness gate passes but one: with the Rust search in place of Fast
Downward, test (a) does not reach the kitchen.

## The integration

The six branches merged with no conflict, since no two components shared a file but the interface crate, which each
had copied byte for byte. The integration then needed:

- one workspace for the seven crates, so that `fmi` links every port;
- one fix: in the workspace, serde_json's insertion-order maps (asked by two crates) reach every crate, and the
  reading cache's key iterated a JSON map's start facts expecting them sorted. It sorts them now. The consumer gate
  G8, run again with the workspace's build, shows no other output depended on map order;
- the reader's memo on its command line (readcore's interface request, in readio's file);
- the real ports in `fmi`: the engine pool, the reader with its exact cache and the memo, the warm planner, the window
  search. An entry the pool does not hold is made from the nearest earlier entry whose movie is a prefix of its own,
  by a fork and the rest of the movie;
- `fmi try` running several tries in one process, the benchmark's warm regime.

## The gates

On the integrated build, with each component's switches off and on:

- The engine's lean build is still exact: fork exactness over 3,000 frames with 7 load children (0 of 6,002 and 0 of
  47,514 comparisons differ), the door and kitchen chains' 37 and 40 exact dumps in each of 7 switch settings, the
  binary state against the JSON dump, and E009's step test (a) through the Python reference on the lean build.
- The reader is byte for byte E009's on the kitchen golden and on ring(38), ring(33) and a refinement, with the memo off
  and on (a refinement stored and then taken whole, a fresh ring(33) reading taken whole from another entry's store).
- The planner's command line is the old binary's byte for byte; its results equal the Python reference's with both
  searches; its store returns the same results; the Rust search gives Fast Downward's lazy h_FF plans on 9 of 9 ring(38)
  goals; the old tail is accepted on all 11 recorded tails.
- The explorer's archive, guide and step checks, and the play component's window tests over the lean engine, pass.
- The 15 tries under the reference settings (strict windows, FD's lama-first) equal the Python reference's in every
  stop, frame, input and every new entry's facts, cold and warm. No python process appeared in a run's process tree.

G10 fails its Gbfs clause. With lazy GBFS and h_FF (the planner component's Rust search, whose plans equal FD's lazy
h_FF plans), test (a)'s plans differ from lama-first's (28 and 13 steps against 30 and 14). The second plan names
event(local:217@18)#9633, whose precondition on state:316 the engine refutes at 3171 (class semantics). The window at
3174 is then empty, the reach extension adds 6 values, and the planner proves there is no plan. With play when ready
and the old tail, the search after the same deviation runs past the harness's 600 s limit. On the 15 tries, the fast
settings keep every stop class and take another route in 3 tries; every new entry they make verifies from boot. A
valid plan of the reading's task is not always a playable route, and FD's lama-first picks one that is on test (a).

## The benchmark

E009's 15 kitchen tries were rebuilt first with the Python reference (B0): each gives the E009 log's return frame,
reading decision, stop, frames, refinements and new entries. Then the Rust tries started from B0's entries, in four
regimes, interleaved try by try with the reference timed again on the same load (B4):

| | median of 15 | ring(33) | ring(38) | sum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| before | 29.4 s | 186.2 s | 23.8 s | 1,780 s |
| one process per try, reference settings | 5.2 s | 63.3 s | 4.2 s | 566 s |
| one process per try, fast settings | 4.4 s | 38.3 s | 2.8 s | 379 s |
| one resident process, reference settings | 3.8 s | 44.6 s | 2.3 s | 414 s |
| one resident process, fast settings | 3.8 s | 17.5 s | 1.1 s | 241 s |

By part, in the resident process with the fast settings:

- A return is a fork of the kept entry (0.55-0.84 ms; 2.2 s before).
- The idle measurement takes 0.2 s (8.3 s before).
- A reused reading opens in 2-5 ms; before, the strict setup decoded every action of a ring(33) reading in 75-114 s.
- A ring(38) reading takes 1.7-3 s fresh (13 s before).
- A ring(33) reading takes 34 s fresh (124 s before), 11 s for a refinement from another entry's memo, 5-8 ms from the
  exact cache.
- The ring(33) planner takes 16 s for the first request of each new goal on the refined reading, 3.2 s when the goal
  repeats; FD's lama-first takes 37-46 s on the same tries.
- Play is 0.4 s on ring(33) tries; test (a)'s 154 s window takes 10.8 s in ready mode.

The reading is 53% of the 241 s (97 s of it the first try's cold readings) and the planner 45%. Engine time has
almost left the try: the frame inside a command costs 36-52 µs against 0.4 ms before, the door chain replays from boot
in 0.33-0.43 s against 2.3-3.1 s, and a fork plus quit costs 0.66-0.77 ms.

## Against the prediction

The engine's numbers came inside or under every predicted range. The reader came at 42% of the old CPU on a fresh
ring(33) reading (predicted 45-60%), the memo's refinement in 11 s (6-15 s), a repeat in milliseconds. The planner's
ring(38) request came in range, its ring(33) request for another start in range and for a new goal above it. Play
missed its prediction for test (a)'s third window (10.8 s against 1-5 s): an observed child frame still costs four
round trips. The warm median of the 15 tries came at 3.8 s against the predicted 0.5-2 s, because the median try is a
fresh ring(38) reading; the ring(33) median (17.5 s) came inside the predicted 8-35 s. The predicted answer, "partly",
is what came.

## Next

Round 2 goes, by the stop rule, to the reading (53%) and the planner (45%): the reader as a library in the one
process with its sink split over threads; the planner's goal-independent reductions kept per reading; an exact,
cheaper h_FF. The Gbfs clause of G10 needs FD's lama-first search in Rust, with plans byte for byte FD's, before the
fast settings can play the reference's routes. The play component's interface requests (one round trip per observed
child frame, batched hit tests) and the try's own time (80 ms against 10 ms) are the smaller items.
