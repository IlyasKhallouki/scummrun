# 2026-10-24: E003 step 4, how big the planner's task is and why

Question: why does relevance keep almost every action of the kitchen task, and how do the reading and
the task grow as the scope takes in more rooms from Part I's real start?

Sources: reader.py 94d495853f07, model.py cc630446bdbe (sha1 prefixes). Results in
`experiments/E003-path-through-cloud/results/task-size-kitchen.log` and `task-size-scaling.{jsonl,log}`;
an independent re-derivation confirmed the numbers below.

## The kitchen task (rooms 28 and 41, 33 global scripts, start S1#7's pre state)

71,729 actions, 716 facts, 583,670 operators after variant expansion, 163.8 MB. Relevance keeps 71,324
actions (99.4%).

- No single fact keeps everything. The sentence script's own bookkeeping does: its slot (script:2), its
  freeze flag, its repeat counter (Var[102]) and the input state (VAR_USERPUT). They back each other up:
  blocking any one alone removes at most 4,668 actions; blocking the three largest together still leaves
  39,056.
- Guessed writes are not the cause: not counting them as achievers removes 30 actions and 60 operators.
- One unit dominates: room 28's local 220 (the pirate leaders' dialog) gives 457,876 of the 583,670
  operators (78.4%), from 26,370 actions.
- By kind: events 255,302 operators, Esc records 226,618, sentences 95,436.
- Multipliers: guessed values x4.09 (mostly dialog verb slots local 220 guesses), precondition value sets
  x2.03, per-wait-point lists of locals x5.1 on events and x6.7 on Esc records, the frozen-slot sentence
  variants x2.

What each lever leaves, alone, from 583,670 operators:

| lever | operators | rule |
|---|---:|---|
| fold forks that end in a yield into conditional effects | 64,240 | plan step 1 already says so; the reader wrote one action per path |
| fold forks whatever their end | 44,618 | changes step 1's rule for handlers that end early |
| every guess through pick variables | 142,913 | a constant in task.py |
| only actions usable from the start | 232,209 | breaks step 4's "translated once" |
| the first, third and fourth together | 18,556 | |

The strict fold was checked on 58,932 paths (two states each): the folded action applies exactly when one
of its paths applies, with the same writes, 0 mismatches. lama-first finds the same 7-step plan on it in
12.8 s, against 171.2 s on the unfolded task.

## Growth with scope (cloud VM, 16 workers, start = boot seed 1 in room 33, goal = the Part I end)

Scope k = the rooms within k constant room changes of room 33 in the scripts (no route data), plus the
global scripts they reach.

| k | rooms | units | records | actions | effects | read wall s | read CPU s | peak memory |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 28 | 878 | 816 | 5,631 | 1 | 5 | 0.2 GB |
| 1 | 4 | 140 | 237,262 | 236,678 | 13,513,842 | 1,163 | 7,131 | 16.1 GB |
| 2 | 10 | 283 | 500,605 when cut at 60 min | | | >3,603 | >15,473 | 52.4 GB |

- Scopes 0 and 1 hold no write of the goal facts, so relevance keeps nothing; scope 2 is the first that
  does (room 83's local 200).
- Writing scope 1's full task fails: `pos:local:200 := 10` is outside its domain. Global 47 starts the
  script numbered by Var[193], which can be a room-local 200 in rooms outside the scope; the reader writes
  the position without checking its domain.
- In scope 2 the 16 CPUs were busy 4.3 on average: most of the time one to three workers ran a few long
  jobs while the rest waited.
- Two-point power laws give the full game (83 rooms, 1,172 units) 3.6M to 4.7M records, 143 to 257 hours
  of reading on 16 workers, and 245 to 315 GB for the reading process at scope 1's 69 KB per record
  (scope 2 already used about 110 KB per record). The exponents disagree between steps (records 3.5 from
  k=0 to 1, 1.3 from k=1 to 2), so these are orders of magnitude, not predictions.

By step 4's rule (stop if the task cannot be built in 32 GB), the full-game task as the reader works today
cannot be built. The levers above shrink the written task; they do not touch the reading, which is where
scope 2 stopped. Decision D7 moves the reading and the task building to Rust.
