## What the first runs showed, and the fixes

Each fix has its guarantee in window-search.md, written before its code. The earlier A4 work's design is kept
otherwise: macro steps by interference, a fork at every frame from the frame after the previous input landed, the
bound b, the stop, the goal only from the plan's goal records, 7 children at a time.

1. **The end rule fired on the plan's own writes.** The kitchen's second window (walk_to(428), after the door's
   sentence) ended one frame after its start with one child: the previous input's sentence changed a fact the next
   input reads. Fix (Lemma 7): the window ends where the input's own precondition closes, and only once every record
   that precedes the input has matched in the idling parent.
2. **An earlier input's record could never match.** An input's record matched only in its own child, so a later window
   that needs it could never succeed. Fix: it matches in every branch of a later window.
3. **The engine runs events the plan leaves out.** At the room entry the engine showed one of the new room's local
   scripts one wait point past the record's prediction, in every child: 1,576 of the first 1,600 ended as mismatches.
   Fix (Lemma 8, the triangle table's kernel): a match checks the writes that a later plan step reads before any later
   step writes them, the goal's facts and the record's own slot. The mismatch rule checks only the slots a pending
   step reads.
4. **After the input took effect, the plan's prediction can still be wrong.** At the same entry, a slot the plan leaves
   alone started one of three other local scripts (local 201, 208 or 209, varying between children), and a later step
   reads their state. No child could succeed, and the window ran to its 3,600-frame end. Fix: such a child is a
   deviation. Its end point is the first frame after its stop where the engine takes a click again, and the harness
   replans from there. Ranking deviations by their stop frame first chose a cutscene that put ego back outside the bar,
   591 frames before a click could land.
5. **Lemma 4′.** b also follows the slots an event starts, so a room's timer that the room entry starts counts.
6. **Lemma 9.** A click's record shows in the frame its last click lands or the next one: the slots it leaves at wait
   points stand there. A child where it does not show, and where no other record of the same click (the reading's
   action lines, by `source`) shows a change a later step reads, is spent at once instead of at the idle stop. Two
   corrections came from the runs. The door's sentence showed one frame after the click, so the check looks at two
   frames. A wait that the plan's next record resumes from may not block (a walk to a door ego already stands at), so
   only the record's final slot writes are checked. The first version stopped all 3,500 children of that window.
7. **The view.** replay.view walked every unit of the scope on every frame, 9 ms of 17 ms per frame. `window.FastView`
   gives the same values on the window's keys only; a test compares it with replay.view.
8. **A start from run B's inputs.** reader-scope `--start-rows`, read-rust `--start-rows`, cmd_run's `starter` and the
   instrument `runb_start.py`, all off by default; the reach extension reads again from such a start.

## The search's cost and memory

- Peak memory of the search: the forks' Pss at most 77 MB with at most 17 live; the harness at most 358 MB (k = 2,

- On the kitchen from boot, 27,827 of the 31,150 child frames, and 232 of the 274 Python CPU s, are the window that

  chosen child). The stepping stones' trials took 80 of the 264 engine CPU s.
- Per child frame, on the kitchen from boot: 8.8 ms of the harness's Python (the state dump's JSON, facts.py, the
  matcher) and 5.9 ms of engine CPU, the dump included. The parent's own per-frame round trip is a small part: one
  fork and one frame per window frame, against 31,150 child frames.
- Lemma 9 cut the first S1#7 window from 12,148 child frames to 1,475 (251 s and 66 s wall, at loads near 20 and
  near 10).

## The Part I projection

Search CPU per input: 76.9 s (kitchen from boot), 13.0 s (from S1#7 pre), 32.5 s (bar door), 34.1 s (k = 2); 44.6 s
over all 18. Part I's ground truth has 250 moves: 0.9 to 5.3 CPU h, 3.1 at the mean. At the plan's 200 inputs:
0.7 to 4.3 CPU h. That is above the plan's kill (2) at 1.5 CPU h. The plan's remedy, a bridge command that forks at each
of the next N frames, removes the parent's round trips, which are not where the time goes. What the measurements point
at: doing a click's approach once in the parent and windowing only the click (a change of what a macro step is, which
needs its own lemma), and less Python per child frame.
