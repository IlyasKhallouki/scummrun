## Surprises

- **The wait decides the kitchen.** r11 saw every object of room 28 and never entered room 41. The door opens on the
  cook's timer, so a click on it works only after a wait. Keeping the waited state was not enough either: by the
  time it was kept, its atoms were old where ego stood. Trying the inputs again from the waited state, inside the
  same expansion, is what passed the door.
- **Position needs three things.** The camera's bucket chains floor steps across a wide room; the walk box and the
  visible objects say when a step showed something. With only the last two (r3-r8), no step was ever new.
- **The sentence line follows the pointer.** After a click on an object the line names it, so every state looked new.
  Parking the pointer on a point that is no verb, object or actor removes the text.
- **Half-made states must not hold the world.** In room 52 object states change by themselves. A hash that held them
  made each floor click a new half-made state (126 a round).
- **One state's trials side by side is slow.** s1 ran one state at a time with its trials on 7 lanes and reached a
  quarter of r12's rate. Batches of states (s2 on) keep the order fixed without that cost.
- **A reporting error of mine.** During r11 I named room 29 the kitchen. Room 29 is another room; the kitchen is
  room 41 (`data/scripts-dos-cd/index.json`). r11 did not reach room 41.

What each stall was:

- **s4: a carried object does not cross a room.** The stop that picks an object up is new, and so is its first step
  into the next room. In a room whose exit is several floor clicks away, the steps in between hold nothing new and
  are dropped. And two objects picked up in two branches are never held together at a high rank.
- **s5 and vm1-vm2: room 51's talk has no idle frame.** `direct show` on a room-52 stop: a click on object 621 loads
  room 51, and from then on a message blocks the bridge's idle rule (6,400 frames and more) while user input is on.
  Every trial into room 51 ended as "no stop". From vm3 on a stop is also where, 300 frames after the inputs, the
  game takes a click and ego stands.
- **vm3: the count of objects is not the progress.** At 246 s a level-2 stop in room 51 picks the dialog's first line
  three times, gives object 567 to actor 3, and leaves room 51 with `var:195 = 478` (478 more than before). The run
  repeats this at levels 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8. But at level 8 a branch with the same count of objects and no such value
  had already held every room, box and bucket, so the stop that holds `var:195 = 480` found nothing new around it
  and its steps were dropped. By 993 s queues 0-3 were empty at every level.
- **P2 is a flag, not the money.** The rung P2 (`bit:72 = 1`) is first held at level 0, by a dialog in room 51 that
  ends back in room 52. The money (`var:195`) rises 213 s later. The rungs file's label for P2 is looser than its
  condition.

Costs measured on the way:

- One engine alone forks in 1.6-3.0 ms (1.3 ms of it before the parent's reply), runs one frame after a fork in
  0.5-1.0 ms and 100 frames in 2.3-4.6 ms, and dumps its state in 0.3-0.5 ms (`direct forkbench`). The engine's
  process holds 28 MB.
- Room 28's stops each cost about 1,367 clicks (four rounds: the stop and three waits) until the motion rule.

Decision: the line (vm4). A stop's line is the set of objects it has gained and the set it owns now; what is new is
judged among the stops of one line. An object used up opens a line as a gain does, so the stop that comes out of
room 51 with the pot gone starts its own sweep.
