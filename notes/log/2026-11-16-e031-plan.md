# 2026-11-16: E031, the plan for the system to optimise its own route, and the calculated estimate

Question (the PI's): can the system, by itself, turn the route it found (full5) into a fast one, glitches of every
kind allowed, and beat the project's hand-routed 21:01.483? The discovery search stays as it is (high8, high9; the
gate), so the optimizer would be a separate part. This entry records the research, the measurements and the estimate.
Nothing of the optimizer was built or run. Files: `experiments/E031-optimizer/` (plan.md, estimate.py, estimate.md,
stairs.py, research/).

## What was measured (evaluation only: nothing here is an input of an optimizer)

Clock: the Any% clock of the 21:01.483 run, quarter-frames / 239.992156448, from the first key in the logo room to
the end.

- full5's end flag (bit 565) first holds at frame 108,830, tick 657,813: 6,148 frames before the end the search
  reported (114,978; the stop it kept came after the game settled). On the Any% clock full5 is 3:01:50.9.
- At the end flag full5 is 9.0 times the 21:01.483 route (10,965 s against 1,221 s). Of the excess: 56% waiting
  (the game takes input and none comes: the search's probes, calms and waits; idle frames are 50.4% of boot to end),
  36% extra room visits (609 visits against 218 and 241 in the two hand routes), 8% everything else. Walking inside
  the visits both routes make is 249 s over the reference.
- How findable each known glitch is, measured on the hand route's movie (`research/reports/10-...`):
  - the logo speed glitch: one key, a window of 47 boot frames, visible in the status (variable 19), pays from the
    next frame;
  - luck by delay: the random state moves every frame at menus and on about half the frames elsewhere; a fixed
    continuation survives a delay 3 to 16% of the time, so a look-ahead of one step is needed;
  - the early opening of the safe: the search found it; the lever left is the entry frame;
  - the slide state itself: a window of 20 frames at talk speed 85 (5 at 255), 2 of 72 verb-slot pairs, 4.6% of the
    floor points, automatic skips off; its only signal is the input counter, so a test of progress does not see it;
  - every use of the slide: chains of 3 or 4 inputs with windows of 1 to 4 frames on at least one link; the first
    large saving comes 73 goals and 127 s after the setup.

## The calculated estimate (`estimate.py`, seed 31; `estimate.md`)

A model T = R - G on the Any% clock over 17 measured segments: R is the route brought toward the hand route without
the slide (28:01.4): re-timing, the intro, re-routing, execution inside visits, the frame length, the draws, the
closing menus; G is what is gained beyond it: luck, tuning, walking, the slide and each skip that needs it. Every
parameter is tagged measured, derived or assumed; a Monte Carlo over the ranges and the stages' successes gives the
odds. An audit corrected it in place (a pool counted twice, a delay rate never measured, upper ends below hand
quality, stages shared by R and G, correlated successes added): `research/reports/12-estimate-audit.md`.

| odds of under 60:00 | 44.6% | 48.4% |
| odds of under 30:00 | 7.4e-06% | 0.00117% |
| odds of under 21:01.483 | 0 | 0 |

Under 21:01.483 occurs in no pair of 150,000 x 150,000 draws (the best pair: 24:37.5). It appears only with every
assumption at its optimistic end: 2.18% without track 6 when the stages succeed independently, 13.2% when they
succeed or fail together (rho 0.9). In 4.44% of draws the closing menus are never answered and there is no time.

What the model does not hold, by its own statement: a route structure better than the hand route's, and glitches
nobody has found (both set to zero); most probabilities of the slide's chain are guesses multiplied about ten deep
(the whole chain: 0.0377% at the centre, worth about 2.5 minutes). The audit's main doubt: the zero is set mostly by
the model's structure, not by its data. The PI's expectation before the estimate was "much more" than the 1 in 10 I
had guessed; the model supports neither.

## The designs and the plan

Three designs made independently (the found movie tightened; a plan over a graph of what the search explored; a new
search with time as cost) were scored by three judges (feasibility, the research's claims, speedrun practice); all
three chose the first as the spine: `research/reports/11-designs-and-judges.md`. Its floor is about 27 to 32 minutes:
it keeps the search's 257 progress steps in their order. The plan (`plan.md`) has ten milestones, each a verified
result; the model's times if each and all before it work (track 6 present): M1 re-timing about 1:34 to 1:43, M2 the
boot (intro skip, frame length) 1:20:59, M3 loops and no-gain drops 1:12:07, M4 direct walks 1:00:25, M5 drops and
unneeded progress 47:30, M6 and M7 order, execution and luck 44:20, M8 the glitches 44:08, M9 a clean run from the
game files. M0 measures the estimate's largest assumptions first. Its own count of the work: about 33 days of
building and 45 laptop hours of runs.
