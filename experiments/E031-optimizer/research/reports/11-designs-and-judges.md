# The three designs and the judges (2026-11-16)

Three architects designed the optimizer independently; three judges scored them. All three judges chose the first design as the spine of the plan.

## Design: Graph-first optimizer (E031-G): what the search explored becomes a measured time-cost graph; plan over it, build the plan in the engine, then tighten

**Thesis.** The search's tree is a learned model of the game's progress, not a route. Its useful content is about 257 progress trials (each a sentence that changed facts), a table of 765 measured walks keyed by where ego entered, and a record of which gains came before which. Its alternative branches gain under 1%. So the optimizer does not search over the tree. It builds a model from it, with no hand-written part. Facts are engine atoms some trial changed, with keys that change by themselves masked out. Actions are the trials' sentences and their fact differences. Costs are measured from one control return to the next, in ticks, keyed by entry. Preconditions start safe (every fact held when the action was seen) and are relaxed by drop tests in the engine. Draws and timers are measured blocks: the model gives them a minimum duration and a cost range over delays, never a predicted outcome. On this model the system plans the cheapest order and path: first the fixed order with direct travel, then only the needed facts, then reordering with learned precedence and timer waits filled by other work. It builds each plan step in the engine and finds each step again on the spot: sentence realisation, then offer trials, then the frozen search as a subprocess for a corridor. Each failure goes back into the model as a learned precondition or a re-measured cost, as in Part I's measure-and-replan loop. Glitches are extra edges and node modes. A frame-length change multiplies frame-paced costs. A teleport or an out-of-order room entry is a new travel edge. A slide-like input counter is a mode flag with learned actions that keep it alive or end it. Their value is what the planner gains with the edge minus the setup cost, and only a replay from boot accepts it. Order of work: first make the clock and re-timing right, because model costs are only meaningful in the re-timed regime. Then structure (travel, then cuts, then order). Execution tightening comes last, after the last structural change, because every earlier change re-rolls later draws.

**Components.**

- **C1 clock and verifier (opt-clock)**: Times any movie by the Any% rule: quarter-frames divided by 239.992156448, from the first key in the logo room (or that room's end if no key) to the first frame of the final fade. Converts the bridge's automatic skips into explicit key events and replays with automatic skips off. Writes each version's settings with its time: seed, talk speed, game folder, track-6 condition. Verifies by two launches from boot (bridge and standalone, same frame, qf and digests) plus high9 verify on a one-line stop file at B and at B-1. Reports every result twice: track 6 present against 23:11.320, track 6 absent against 21:01.483. The final-fade test (var 293 == 161 in room 0) lives only in the evaluation script, never in optimizer logic. Reuses: scripts/anypercent_verify.py: its replay() and rta logic, in a parametrised copy taking seed, start event, game folder and goal. The scratch wrappers metric/replay_ref.py and time_movie.py. fmi-shorten movie.rs (launch, replay, verify, write, promote) and skips.rs. high9 verify. experiments/E027-direct/run/gate.sh. New: The parametrised tool (about 300 lines of Python), the explicit-key writer, the start and end readers. A general end for the optimizer: after the search's end flag, the closing menus are answered by C5's dialog-line search, and the run ends at the first room change after which no input is taken for 3,600 ticks (the bridge's stall record).
- **C2 atlas (engine walk of the tree)**: Reads the true state of every one of full5's 58,276 kept stops by walking the tree with forks: each stop is its parent's fork plus its own events. For each stop it stores the digest, the RNG, tick and qf, ego position and box, the entry token (the room entered from, or the last object acted on), camera, input counter, cutscene depth, frame length, and masked world facts as a difference from the parent. The mask is the keys that change in a 200-frame idle fork. Three twin forks with a perturbed RNG mark each progress trial as draw-dependent and name its draw keys. This replaces the files' news, which give only a lower bound on the facts held. Reuses: fmi-engine host.rs fork_bin, fork_each_bin, digest_json, step and block. fmi-shorten label.rs twin, twin_diff and world(). fmi-explore events.rs (reads stop movies and spare lists). The forkbench costs already measured: fork 4.46 ms, facts 0.79 ms. New: A parallel tree walker (6 workers, about 30 CPU-minutes, about 6 minutes wall), a store of differences (full dumps are about 400 kB a stop), the idle-mask and entry-token builders, and per-stop tick and qf. About 800 lines of Rust in a new crate fmi-opt.
- **C3 model builder (the graph)**: Turns the atlas into a planning model. Facts: masked world atoms changed by some trial. Actions: each distinct (room, sentence identity (verb, object a, object b) from the input log, entry token), with add and delete effects and numeric counters. Cost: measured ticks from control to control, minimum and spread. Travel: the measured entry-keyed walks (765 triples, 371 room pairs), each tagged with the fact sets it was seen under, because the room graph changes with progress; unmeasured pairs get an optimistic cost and are queued for measurement. Preconditions: the safe set first; static hints from the scripts' writer and reader map (which script writes or tests an atom, extracted automatically from the dump); then the results of drop tests. Draw blocks: a stretch from a draw-marked trial to the first trial whose outcome its draw keys change (found by twins, including links written early and read late, such as one written at frame 3,081 and read at 20,735). A block is one macro-action with a cost range over delays 0..12 and a rule that any change to the number of draws before it re-values it by realisation. Timer blocks: a wait trial's minimum, found by the padding ladder, becomes a release time ('at least T ticks after event E'). The scheduler may fill that time with other actions. Mode flags on nodes: frame length, input counter above its rest value, ego not in the room. Reuses: guide-end.json key names and the writer map (built automatically from the scripts dump; hints only). fmi-core Facts and Key types. fmi-shorten draws.rs (draw marks) and verdict.rs (conds, changed, ego_at). New: Model types and builder, the entry-keyed travel table with validity sets, block detection, precondition learning (safe, then relaxed by recorded test outcomes), JSON export. About 1,200 lines.
- **C4 planner**: Three solvers, each used only after the one before has a verified result. (a) Fixed order: the found sequence of progress actions, with a Dijkstra over the travel graph between consecutive action rooms at the facts held then. Cost is measured, and optimistic only where unmeasured. (b) Needed facts: backward relevance from the end action over the learned preconditions, which drops actions whose effects no needed precondition uses. Candidates are ranked by the ticks they would save and tested by C5. (c) Order: a sequential ordering problem (asymmetric TSP with precedence) over at most 138 room visits, with precedence from the learned preconditions. It is cut at stops that need every earlier fact. Pieces of 12 or fewer visits are solved exactly by dynamic programming; larger ones by cheapest insertion plus Or-opt. Timer release times are scheduled as gaps to fill. Alternatively the whole model is handed to taskcore (Fast Downward-style search) as a generated SAS task, the Part I precedent (67 actions there, about 250 here, cut into pieces). The planner proposes and ranks. Only the engine decides. Reuses: fmi-plan (taskcore warm planner, check_plan, predict) for the generated-task route. Part I's measure-and-replan loop and its entry-keyed costs (docs/optimization.md). New: Segment Dijkstra, backward relevance, the sequential-ordering solver with timer gaps, a SAS-task generator from C3's model, and the plan cache keyed by model version. About 1,000 lines.
- **C5 realiser (plan to movie, re-finding each step)**: Builds a plan in the engine from boot with forks, one step at a time. Travel step: clicks the exit found by the engine's hit test (the nearest pixel to ego that has the same hit), and checks the entry against the travel table's entry token. On a mismatch it falls back to the approach search. Action step: realises the sentence by identity (fmi-play realize: offer, approach, verb then object, judged by the input log). If that fails: offer trials, then dialog-line search, then skips switched off for the step. If that fails: the frozen high9 is run as a subprocess (--from a one-line stop file, --until the step's first new fact, --stops bound, the leg's edges and toggles), and the stop it finds is spliced in. Each step is judged by the masked facts plus ego position at its next control return (the working equality). Digest equality is counted as an exact rejoin. A failed step becomes a learned precondition (the facts the fork lacked) or a re-measured cost, and the plan is solved again from the last good fork. Reuses: fmi-play realize.rs and executor.rs. fmi-shorten refind.rs (realize, offer trials, dialogs, skips off), scan.rs (fork_each windows), label.rs (place, place_hit, identity, relabel), verdict.rs. fmi-explore afford.rs (offer_cfg, inputs_of). The frozen high9 binary, unchanged. New: The plan executor loop, the travel executor with entry checks, the feedback writer to C3, the stop-file writer and splice for high9 corridors, and the step ledger (route-NNN.jsonl plus steps.jsonl in E024's format). About 1,500 lines.
- **C6 re-timer (forward pass)**: Places each input group at the earliest frame the engine accepts: control-2 to control+1 (this also gives buffering), with inner offsets kept, then verb and object spacing reduced to one frame. A failed group gets, in order: the padding ladder (1/8, 1/4, 1/2, then the whole original gap), a delay scan at draw-marked groups, then restore of the original gaps back to the last digest-equal control return. Group verdict: the same clicks taken with the same identity, the masked facts and draw keys equal at the next control return, and ego at the same place. Runs after every structural change, because the model's costs are only meaningful in the re-timed regime. Also scans the boot inputs: one key per frame before first control, grouped by digest at first control, the best probed over the first 50 groups. Reuses: fmi-shorten pass.rs, scan.rs, verdict.rs (click, cond, earlier), draws.rs. fmi-engine fork_each_bin. Ideas from scripts/anypercent_auto.py (early shifts, pure-shift test). The draft plan's stages 1 and 2 (e031/plan.md). New: The forward pass with the ladder and the restore-to-digest repair, the boot-key scan, and a whole-route check every 50 groups with ddmin over a failing block. About 900 lines.
- **C7 tightener (execution, last)**: On the final structure, from the last draw-marked group onward first, then backward. Exit clicks at the earliest pixel (up to 40 samples on an 8 px grid; stop after 3 identical arrivals). Re-clicks during a walk, by the direction rule, with a beam only where the rule gains. Approach clicks for off-screen targets. Luck by delay at draw-marked groups: delays 0..12 with look-ahead to the group that consumes the draw, keeping the earliest completion, not merely full5's draw. Per-skip scan, report only. Acceptance in three levels: exact rejoin by digest, state equal with the RNG different (delay give-back), and after the last draw one for one. Reuses: fmi-shorten scan.rs. E025's walk probes (direction rule, first point, approach). Ideas from anypercent_auto.py (delay_lookahead, play_steered, reclick). New: Pixel sampler, re-click search, delay look-ahead with twin-derived horizons. About 700 lines.
- **C8 glitch layer (anomaly edges)**: Census: replay the current route with the per-frame log, and record the baseline of (input counter, cutscene depth) pairs, frame-length values at control stops, ego off-room with input on, engine warnings, and ego jumps faster than walking. Each new value is an anomaly, with no threshold to tune. Scans, all by fork_each: (A) one key at each boot frame and at each frame where the frame length is below its value at control; (B) skip timing, Esc at frames 0..60 of each cutscene; (C) the bracket race at visits where a freeze-resistant script with an absolute input-on write is alive (read from the state dump's slots; the opcode filter is derived from the dump, no script numbers). This scan uses skips off, a floor click, a cutscene-opening item reading at lead 0..25 frames and inventory slots, about 14,000 tries a visit. Every anomaly state becomes a graph node with a mode flag. Persistence profile: continue the route's own steps from it and record which actions end the mode, which become the mode's learned delete conditions. Window scan inside the mode: inputs at frames the baseline could not use. The reward is a fact earlier than the model's plan, a room entered out of route order, or ego displaced beyond walking, and each becomes a new edge (cost measured, mode required). Teleports become travel edges; a frame-length change becomes a global cost factor re-measured by C6. Reuses: The bridge's existing Status fields (up, cs, v19), the frame log (SPEEDRUN_FRAME_LOG), run conditions (CondKind::Var), and the state dump's slot and cutscene blocks. fmi-engine fork_each_bin. high9 --from a stop whose movie has keys and skip switches (events.rs accepts them), for valuing anomalies that change what normal play can reach. New: Census, scans A-C, persistence profiler, mode-aware edges in C3 and the planner's mode states. About 1,200 lines. Patch 0014 (read-only anomaly events in forked children) only if scans show anomalies that open and close between stops, and only with the gate's six digests unchanged.
- **C9 driver and ledger**: Runs each milestone as one bounded job (at most 3 h, heavy lock, inhibit lock, watcher). Keeps every version, never overwritten, and appends lab notes in the numbered-experiment format. Runs gate.sh after any build that touches shared crates. The final clean-room run rebuilds everything from the game files alone, from boot, in one chain. Reuses: scripts/vmrun.sh (for the single cloud run, if used), E027's env.sh, the watcher scripts, E024's video tool for rendering any version. New: The fmi-opt CLI (subcommands atlas, model, plan, realise, retime, tighten, glitch, verify) and run scripts. About 400 lines.

**Glitch discovery.** Glitches are found as anomalies against the route's own baseline and valued as graph edges. They are never named. Discovery (C8) has two parts. Signals are any new value, against the census of the verified route, of: (input counter, cutscene depth); frame length at a control stop; ego absent from the room while input is on; an engine warning; ego moving farther than walking allows. On full5 these are silent from first control to the end flag, so they give no false alarms. Scans target the frames the discovery search never touches. (A) One key on each boot frame and each frame where the frame length is below its control value: at most 13,640 tries, which contains the logo glitch. (B) Esc at frames 0..60 of each of the 574 cutscene intervals: about 9,500 tries. (C) The bracket race at the visits where a freeze-resistant script holding an absolute input-on write is alive, read from the state dump's slots: about 14,000 tries a visit, about 1.9 CPU-hours. The probe at stop 9329 already showed input counter 2 at depth 0 for leads 16-20. Valuation has four steps of rising cost. (1) A persistence profile: one replay continuing the route's own steps, recording which actions end the mode; these become the mode's delete conditions in the model. (2) A window scan inside the mode, at frames and inputs the baseline could not use. A find becomes an edge if it gives a fact earlier than the current plan, a room entered out of order (a travel edge not in the table), or a displacement (a teleport edge). Each edge has a measured cost and a required mode. (3) The planner re-plans with the new edges and modes. The edge's value is the planned saving minus its setup and keep-alive cost: a tick debt, so a setup that costs about 22 s is not rejected by a step-local judge. Anomalies that change what normal play reaches are valued by the frozen high9 from a stop file with keys and skip switches (10-20 probes a run). (4) Acceptance only by realisation and replay from boot to the end. A frame-length change is not an edge but a cost factor: C6 re-times the whole route under it and keeps it only if the end comes earlier. Track 6 is not a glitch: it is an evaluation condition, and both conditions are reported. Honesty note for the paper: scans A-C were shaped knowing the reference glitches. The test of generality is to run them unchanged on MI2 and Fate of Atlantis and report what they find.

**Verification.** Every version is a movie from boot (route-NNN.jsonl with its steps.jsonl). It is accepted only after: (1) Two launches from boot, the bridge and the standalone, that agree on frame, tick, qf and the digest at every control return (fmi-shorten movie::verify, with settings read only from the step log's first line). (2) An explicit-key replay with automatic skips off that reaches the same end frame. The Any% rules require raw input alone, one key or mouse event per frame. (3) Timing by the Any% rule: qf/239.992156448 from the first key in the logo room (or that room's end) to the first frame of the final fade. The fade is detected only in the evaluation tool, by the reference's goal (room 0, var 293 == 161), which holds for any final reply. Seed, talk speed and game folder are recorded with the time. Seed 1 is kept, because the Rust host refuses the reference seed. Each result is given twice: track 6 present (compared with 23:11.320) and absent (compared with 21:01.483). It is also given at the search's end flag, against the hand route's 20:21.044. (4) The frozen high9 verify on a one-line stop file at B with the end flag set and at B-1 with it unset. (5) gate.sh PASS whenever a shared crate was rebuilt, so high8 and high9 stay the paper's binaries. Model numbers (planned costs, bounds) are never reported as results. Only verified replays are.

**Risks.**

- The executor (C5) is the crux. Re-making a trial by its sentence identity from another position and state stopped at step 5 of 12 in E024. If identity plus offer trials realise under 60% of steps, M2-M4 fall back to high9 corridors, about 15 min each, and only about 10 fit a run. Then the route stays near M1 plus a few corridors (about 1:10).
- The walk table may not hold at the facts where it is used: the room graph changes with progress (a 3,250-frame spread between admitted-transition variants). Each direct walk needs an engine check, and an optimistic unmeasured cost can mislead the planner until it is measured.
- Hidden state outside the masked facts (script locals, inventory page and order, camera, CD and sound state, the input-on counter). A merge by facts can pass locally and fail later. Only a replay to the end catches this, and ddmin over a failing block costs whole re-times.
- Draws: any cut or reorder shifts the count of draws before later ones. A draw block where the draw decides which inputs are needed (which exit, how many repeats) cannot be fixed by delay. It needs a corridor re-search and can eat the run budget. Luck by delay worked at only 2 of 6 non-zero points in the audit's measurement.
- Interactions: gains from travel, drops and order do not add up (the estimate's iota). Each structural milestone must be measured end to end, not summed.
- The general end rule (closing menus answered, then a stall) may pick a slower final reply or fail. Evaluation can fall back to timing at the end flag against 20:21.044, which loses comparability with 21:01.483.
- Frame length 5 from the logo key may break inner offsets and draw repairs. It must be settled in M1, before structure, or M1 is redone after it.
- Compute and the shared laptop: load averages of 25-30 were measured. The atlas, drop tests and scans fit 3 h jobs, but corridor runs are the cap. The cloud has about one 6 h run left, kept for M7.
- Glitch payoff, not detection, is the hard part. Forming the slide state is likely (80%), but carrying it to its uses is under 5%. Without the slide family or an equally large unknown gain, 21:01.483 is out of reach.
- Claims of generality: the scan shapes were designed knowing the glitches. They must be run unchanged on other v5 games before any claim is made.

**Final estimate.** Expected result (the median of what I expect to verify from boot): about 55 min with track 6 absent, about 57 min with it present, on the Any% clock. The likely path: M1 (about 1:22) and M2 succeed in part, about 1:00-1:05. M3 cuts some unneeded steps. M5 and M6 add 2-5 min. The 10th to 90th percentile is about 44 min to 1:25. Odds, track 6 absent: under 60 min, about 55%; under 30 min, about 2%; under 21:01.483, about 0.3%. With track 6 present, each is slightly lower (about 50%, 1.5%, 0.2%).

What my design realises from the calculated estimate, in this order:
- Re-timing (r_xwait, r_inner) and the logo boot key, in M1.
- Direct walks (v_walk) and the executor (p_exec), measured first in M2.
- Drops of no-news steps and unneeded progress (q_atom, p_drop, y_need), in M3.
- Reordering (y_order), in M4.
- In-visit execution, luck by delay, walking and tuning, in M5.
- Slide formation and its chain, in M6.

Where I differ, and why:
1. My median is about 6 min lower than the audited estimate's (1:01:05 track 6 absent). The graph schedules timer waits as release times and fills them with other needed work; the estimate only removes waits (r_xwait) and never overlaps them. Backward relevance over learned preconditions also targets the 101 progress trials that set only keys no script tests, which the estimate's y_need centre (0.30 of a 582 s pool) treats as mostly fixed.
2. My under-30 odds (about 2% against 0.0012%) are higher. The Monte Carlo's peaked ranges and its structural cap (nothing better than the hand route's structure, no unknown glitches) make the tail too thin. A learned dependency graph can find a needed-fact set smaller than the found route's in ways the estimate's shares cannot express, and the anomaly scans can find a gain not on the hand list. Neither is likely, but neither is near zero.
3. On 21:01.483 I agree: it needs hand-quality structure and the whole slide chain, or about 1,400 s of unknown gain. I put it under 0.5%, not 0, only for those structural unknowns.

The estimate's dominant parameters (the executor and the walk realisation) are also my dominant risks. M2's first 30-step measurement decides whether the design's middle milestones are reachable, and it should be run before anything else is built beyond M0 and M1.

## Design: Movie-first optimizer (architect stance: keep full5's order, make that one movie as fast as possible, then push it as far as it honestly goes)

**Thesis.** Treat full5's movie as the only route skeleton. Every pass either (a) moves existing inputs in time, (b) deletes existing inputs, or (c) replaces a stretch of the movie with inputs the frozen search or a named general scan made, and every input in a kept version carries provenance back to one of those sources. The biggest new idea relative to the draft plan: most of the 'direct walks' pool can be had by LOOP ERASURE of the movie (cut every stretch whose two ends are the same state by masked facts plus ego position, verified in the engine), which needs no executor that re-makes a trial from another place. That removes the calculated estimate's most important single assumption (p_exec) from the largest pool. The research says the path's own returns to an already-held state hold 53,662 frames (37,426 without padding), against 5,599 for everything off the path. A loop A->...->A usually re-enters A by the same exit, so ego stands where the next group expects it, and the movie's clicks stay valid. Order of passes: true end and clock (with the closing menus answered) -> re-time -> boot (intro skip and logo key) -> loop erasure and no-gain drops -> splices of measured tree walks and short frozen-search corridors -> drop tests on progress trials -> execution tightening and luck -> glitch windows along the movie. Floor: with the found order and the found actions fixed, the 257 progress trials alone are 20,970 frames (35.2 min at 6 ticks a frame, about 29 min at the logo glitch's 5). With best measured walks between them and all unneeded progress dropped, movie-first bottoms out at about 27-32 min. It cannot reach 21:01.483. That needs a different set of actions (the slide's skips replace progress steps with other actions in another order), reordering of gains (worth 3,000-7,000 frames) and cheaper solutions to puzzles than the ones the search found. Those belong to a re-routing architecture, not to this one. Movie-first's job is to get to about 45 min quickly and robustly, to give a verified, bounded baseline that every other design must beat, and to deliver the measured tools those designs need: the clock, the state table, the re-timer, the drop rounds and the anomaly census.

**Components.**

- **C1 anyclock: Any% clock and verifier**: Times any movie on the reference's clock: qf from the first key delivered in the logo room (or the logo room's end if no key) to the first frame of the final fade, /239.992156448. Converts the bridge's automatic skips to explicit key events and replays with automatic skips off, as rules/anypercent.md requires. Replays every version from boot in two hosts (Rust high9 verify and the October Python controller on build/svm-direct) and checks the frame, qf and trace digests. ## Design: RACE: a cost-ordered Go-Explore archive over full5's progress chain (search-first optimizer, separate crate fmi-race)

**Thesis.** The re-timing, intro, direct-walk, drop, reorder, luck, logo and glitch stages are all one operation: from a known state, try an input sequence and keep the result if it reaches a state the archive already has, but earlier on the Any% clock. So build one search and make the stages its operator families. This is Go-Explore with cost. A cell is (true facts with self-changing keys masked, room, anomaly signature). Each cell keeps only its fastest trajectory, measured in quarter-frames from the start event. Returning to a cell is exact (fork or deterministic replay), so the archive never holds a trajectory that did not run. A wrong merge can only lose options. It can never produce a false result.

The found route makes this fit the laptop.
- full5's re-read path seeds the archive and orders it into layers of progress. Selection walks the layers front to back as a beam with archive dominance, not as open-ended novelty.
- Operators are mostly full5's own winning trials (257 progress trials, plus the in-room steps re-admitted when a drop fails) and its measured entry-keyed walks. That is tens of operators per cell, not the frozen search's verb x object x point fan-out. The frozen search spent 9.7 M trials and 82,422 worker-seconds on discovery. A RACE pass is about 0.5 M engine attempts and 4-6 CPU-hours.
- Every operator is realised at the earliest frame the game accepts (control-2..control+1). Re-timing is therefore built into every expansion rather than a separate stage.
- Glitches enter the same way. Anomaly generators are just more operators: keys at frames where the frame length differs from its rest value, Esc timing, skips switched off with a cutscene-opening input raced against a running input bracket, and inputs while input is on inside a cutscene. An anomaly signature splits a cell, so a glitch state survives in the archive until it earns an earlier arrival at some later layer or runs out its tick debt.

How it differs from the frozen discovery search (which it never touches):
- Objective: quarter-frames to the Any% end, not novelty or level.
- Equality: engine-read masked facts, not line and flag novelty.
- Dominance: an earlier arrival replaces the stored trajectory and is re-expanded. The discovery search keeps first arrivals.
- Where it acts: at any frame, including keys, skip switches, inside cutscenes and mid-walk. The discovery search clicks only at an idle stop with cutscene depth 0 and automatic skips.
- Operators come from that search's output.
- Code: it lives in a new workspace member. It may call `high9 --from` only as a capped subprocess rescue.

It realises the calculated estimate's mechanisms in this order:
1. Closing menus and clock.
2. Re-timing and the intro.
3. Direct walks and drops.
4. Unneeded progress and order.
5. Logo glitch, draw delays and the interaction effects.
6. In-visit execution and walking.
7. The slide family as a stretch lane.

Honest bottom line: this is a better way to realise the estimate's pools, not a way around its structural ceiling. My median is about 58 min with track 6 removed. Beating 21:01.483 stays well under 1%.

**Glitch discovery.** Glitches are found by the same mechanism as faster routes. The anomaly signature is part of the cell key, and anomaly generators are operators. Novelty needs no threshold. On full5 the signals are silent where measured (6 counter/depth pairs, the counter never at 2, 0 frames with input on inside a cutscene, 0 frames with ego off-room, 0 warnings), so any new value is new.

Generators, each found by engine observation or by opcode kind, with no game number:
- G1: keys and Esc at frames where the frame length is off its rest value, and over boot. This finds the logo glitch in 375 to 13,640 tries. It must be judged in quarter-frames, not frames.
- G2: Esc and text-skip offsets inside the 574 cutscene intervals (about 19,000 tries).
- G3: the bracket race (skips off, floor click, cutscene-opening input k frames later). It runs at stops where a live freeze-resistant script holds an absolute input-on; that is 32 route visits x about 14,000 tries, about 1.9 CPU-hours.
- G4: inputs while input is on inside a cutscene.

Valuation is late and comparative. An anomaly cell keeps a tick debt against the best normal cell of its layer, and a cheap persistence profile goes first: it continues its own trajectory and counts the layers the anomaly survives. Only survivors are expanded. A survivor is kept if a descendant reaches some layer earlier than any normal cell, or reaches a progress fact out of route order. That second condition is how a sequence break shows up without being named. The prize for a sequence break is read from the route: the quarter-frames between the skipped layers.

Timing-only effects such as draw steering and the necklace-type animation skip need no anomaly cell. They appear as earlier arrivals at ordinary cells through delay and offset operators.

Honest limits:
- G1 and G3 were shaped knowing that the logo glitch and the slide exist. The generality check therefore runs them unchanged on MI2 and Fate of Atlantis, and reports what they find.
- Carrying the slide into Part III needs 6-10 timed steps. The cell carry (anomaly signature kept across rooms) is the only lever, and I put it at about 5%.
- The track 6 card is a files condition, not a glitch. It is reported as an evaluation condition, never searched.

**Verification.** Archive scores are always provisional. A milestone result exists only after C0 has done the following:
1. Export the winning trajectory as one explicit movie: every automatic skip written as a key, at most one key and one mouse event per frame, skip switches as events.
2. Replay it from boot with automatic skips off, on build/svm-direct (patches 0001-0013), and separately with frozen high9 verify. Both must reach the same end frame.
3. Time it on the Any% clock. Clock: qf divided by 239.992156448, the same arithmetic that gives 21:01.483 = 302,746 qf for the reference. Start: the first key delivered in the logo room, or that room's exit if there is none. End: the first frame of the final fade, found by the generic end rule and cross-checked at evaluation only with the reference goal (room 0 and var 293 == 161), which holds whatever the final reply.
4. Repeat the replay and require byte-identical traces.
5. Record seed 1, talk speed, game folder and engine build.

Every result is reported twice: with track 6 present against 23:11.320, and with track 6 removed against 21:01.483. The archive's own qf must equal the verifier's to the frame; any difference fails the milestone and is logged as a bug. Inside the search, acceptance is local, with three levels: exact digest rejoin, masked facts plus the next draw-marked step, and full replay at the end. Only a full replay from boot to the fade counts.

After every build: gate.sh PASS, and the sha256 of high8 and high9 unchanged. The known Any% route and runs are read only by the report script (the stretch-by-stretch comparison), never by fmi-race. A grep check in CI enforces that no path under routes/anypercent, docs/anypercent or runs/anypercent, and no numeric game id literal, appears in the race crate.

**Risks.**

- The executor (p_exec) decides everything, as in the estimate. If re-making trials by name from another place fails often, walks and reordering collapse back to full5's order. Mitigations: measure 30 trials in M1 before building M2, and the high9 --from rescue (capped at 6 calls a run, 6-15 min each). It is also the first stop rule: below 0.5 at M1, the design falls back to the draft plan's stage pipeline.
- Masked-fact merging can join states that differ in hidden state: script locals, RNG, inventory order, camera, CD state. This cannot produce a false result, because trajectories are real. It can prune the only working branch. Mitigation: K=2 per cell keyed by entry token, and nogoods recorded with the full digest so a pruned twin can come back.
- Random draws: a faster prefix reshuffles every later draw, so stretches ruled by draws (fights, the catacomb-like room with 21 visits, a combination drawn about 17,650 frames before its use) can fail in every delay variant. Each one then costs a rescue call; more than about 10 such sites exceed the rescue budget.
- Compute: if attempts cost about 0.3 s instead of 0.1 s (long walks, draw look-aheads, a shared laptop at load 25-30), a pass is 15 CPU-h. Then B drops to 12 and w to 6, and the M3 and M4 gains shrink.
- The heuristic h is not admissible once drops work. The beam can prune the ordering that would be best. This is accepted: the result is an upper bound, not an optimum.
- The frame-length-5 re-run (M4) may break operators whose waits count jiffies. It is a full re-run, so M3 must succeed first and M4 doubles M3's run cost.
- The generic end rule may pick a wrong end (a loop that looks like credits). The cross-check against the evaluation goal catches this, but the system must not depend on it.
- Interactions: gains measured stage by stage do not add up (the estimate's iota). Because RACE measures only whole trajectories, this shows up early rather than at the end.
- Talk speed and skips-off are settings the slide lane needs. Changing either invalidates the whole archive, so the slide lane can only run as its own full pass.

**Final estimate.** The expected final time with track 6 removed: median about 58 min (with track 6 present about 1:00); 10th to 90th percentile about 42 min to 1:35.

Odds:
- under 60 min: about 50% (track 6 removed), 46% (present)
- under 30 min: about 2%
- under 21:01.483: about 0.3% (effectively out)

How this relates to the calculated estimate, which I build on:
- I take its structure: R toward hand no-slide structure, minus G gains, with medians held back mainly by p_exec, v_walk and drop success. Its post-audit median is 1:01:05 (track 6 removed) and 1:03:20 (present); its centre if every stage succeeds is about 41:42.
- I am about 3 min faster at the median, for two reasons. First, RACE performs drops, unneeded-progress removal and ordering as one search with learned precedence and earliest-per-cell propagation. That raises the realised shares of q_atom, y_need and y_order over a ddmin-then-reorder pipeline. Second, the rescue path through the frozen search raises the low end of p_exec.
- I give up some walking polish (v_walk's fine part and e_walk), because compute goes to the search, not to deep corridor search.
- Under 30 min I put at about 2%, against the model's 0.0012%. The model caps the route's structure at the hand route's. A search over progress operators can find some of the 257 trials unnecessary, which goes below the model's 35-min progress floor, but only if many are droppable, and the graph report's evidence (101 trials set only keys no script tests) is suggestive, not measured.
- Under 21:01.483 I put at about 0.3%, against the model's 0 in its central draws and 2-13% only in its optimistic corner. It needs both hand-quality structure and the slide chain carried into Part III (about 5% by itself), and these are not independent successes.

What would move the estimate fastest, all measured in M1-M2 within the first about 10 days: p_exec on 30 trials; the share of the about 200 direct walks valid in the engine; 50 drop tests on the atom-changing no-news steps; and the 257 progress-trial drop tests.
