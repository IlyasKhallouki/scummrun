# Execution-level optimisation

A research report made for E031's plan (2026-11-16) by a reading agent; its claims were used in the plan after the plan's critics checked them. Evidence paths are as the agent gave them.

## Findings

### 1. Where full5's time goes, frame by frame (first control to bit 565): half of it is the game waiting for input, 30% is ego walking under player control, 15% is input switched off. This sets what execution-level work can and cannot reach.

- **Evidence:** Per-frame replay /var/tmp/fast-mi/direct/video-full5/run/eng/frames.jsonl, frames 2,729-108,830 (106,102 frames, 641,492 ticks). Idle with control: 55,261 frames, 331,572 ticks, 51.7%. Player walking (mv != 0, input on, no cutscene): 31,801 frames, about 190,800 ticks, 30%. Input off (cutscenes and scripted stretches): 16,084 frames, 101,378 ticks, 15.8%. Messages, sentence queue, fades: 2,956 frames. So 50,841 frames (309,920 ticks, 86 min) are the game being busy. Scripts: /var/tmp/fast-mi/e031-research/execopt2/w1.py, i1.py, t1.py.
- **Why it matters:** Execution-level levers on the same route give about 3:03 -> 1:30 (range 1:19 to 1:46), near 1:20 with the logo glitch on top. That is about -50%, not -10% and not the draft's -28%. The floor of this route under perfect execution is about 65-70 min, because the hand route's whole 21:01 run is 14,820 frames against 50,841 busy frames here. Beating 21:01 needs the route itself to lose about 70% of its busy frames; input timing cannot do that.

### 2. Lever 1, earliest input after control returns. Best gain per compute by far. Procedure: one forward pass over the 1,485 control returns; for each input group, fork at the previous completion and place the group's first input at control-2, control-1, control, control+1 (inner offsets kept); keep the earliest that passes; on failure give back 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, all of the original gap. Local test: every click taken with the same identity (input log), and at the next control return the state equals the baseline's (all globals minus keys that change by themselves, bits, owners, classes, object states, room, ego position), earlier. Look-ahead: if twin forks with a perturbed RNG mark the group or a following one as draw-dependent, the test runs to the end of the group that consumes the draw, with a delay scan as repair.

- **Evidence:** 1,485 idle runs hold 55,262 frames; all but one frame per run is 53,777 frames (322,662 ticks, 89.6 min, 50.3% of the route). 350 runs of 70-79 frames hold 25,690 frames (the search's probe plus calm), 98 runs of 80-200 hold 9,258, 11 runs over 200 hold 6,545. 1,988 of 1,994 clicks were issued from an idle frame. The hand route's driver clicks at gate+1 on all 532 moves (src/speedrun/executor.py wait_gate). E024's measured cost: 13.5 ms + 0.034 ms per frame for an accepted candidate, 2.6 ms refused.
- **Why it matters:** Expected gain 85-95% of the pool (a guess; the unknown is how many of the search's 33 wait trials, 8,550 frames, the game needs): -274,000 to -307,000 ticks. Cost: 4 candidates at about 15-20 ms each, 60-80 ms per group, about 2 minutes for the whole route on one engine, at most 30 minutes with repairs. About 2,400 ticks saved per engine-second without repairs.

### 3. Lever 2, verb and object click spacing. Procedure: inside a group, move each later input to one frame after the one before it; also try a right click on the object when it queues the same sentence (one frame instead of two). Local test: same sentence in the input log (verb, objects) and the next control return earlier by the frames removed. No look-ahead beyond lever 1's.

- **Evidence:** 581 pairs of consecutive clicks have only idle frames between them: 334 at a gap of 3 frames, 247 at a gap of 9; removable 2,644 frames (15,864 ticks, 2.5%). This sits inside lever 1's pool, so it is not added twice. Engine rule (docs/research/anypercent-engine-mechanics.md 6.1-6.3): one click per frame, a verb key overwrites a click on the same frame, so verb plus object costs 2 frames at least; the hand route used 8 right clicks. The 1,921 pointer-parking moves sit one frame after each click and must be dropped between consecutive clicks (one mouse event per frame, rules/anypercent.md).
- **Why it matters:** About 2,000-2,600 frames for about 20 s of compute (2-3 candidates per pair). It is the second cheapest gain and needs no new mechanism beyond lever 1's pass.

### 4. Lever 3, buffering inputs before control returns. Procedure: lever 1's candidates at control-2, control-1 and control are this lever. A verb key or click on the frame the gate opens counts, because the engine reads clicks and verb keys after that frame's scripts; after a cutscene ends the click is cleared, so control+1 is the earliest there. Local test: a pure shift (the group completes exactly that many frames sooner in the same state).

- **Evidence:** scripts/anypercent_auto.py --early and Executor._play_early (shifts 2 then 1, a lag snapshot 2 frames back, a state signature). Measured by comparing runs/anypercent/i2a/results.jsonl (no early inputs) with runs/anypercent/m2/results.jsonl: 39 of 248 goals (Part II to the end) finished 1 or 2 frames sooner, 56 frames, 4.66 s. That is 0.23 frames per goal. The hand route had to flag 9 moves no_early because the earlier frame changed a later random draw.
- **Why it matters:** Scaled to full5's 1,485 groups: about 340 frames (2,000 ticks, 34 s); on a 530-move route about 10 s. Small, nearly free inside lever 1. The 9 hand flags are what the twin-fork draw marks must replace.

### 5. Lever 4, text and talk speed: nothing to gain at a fixed route. The bridge's automatic skip is already at the per-line floor, and the talk speed is already the fastest and has no effect on skipped lines.

- **Evidence:** full5's trace: 1,834 text skips and 211 cutscene skips after first control. 1,819 of the 1,834 were pressed with the line's timer at 54 of 60 jiffies, the first frame a key can act (keys are read before the frame's scripts print the line). Consecutive skips in a run are 1 frame apart (371) or 2 frames apart (556). Text runs hold 4,048 frames (3.8%). var 37 = 0 at segment start (text speed 255). The hand run pressed 338 text skips in its whole 23:11 run.
- **Why it matters:** Lines cost 1-2 frames each and only a route with fewer lines lowers that. Per-skip choice is worth a report-only scan (skip off for one line, judged at the next control return; 2,045 skips at about 20 ms, 40 s), as a guard against an Esc that changes state and as a luck knob. For an Any% claim the automatic skips must be written out as explicit key presses and replayed with the automatic skips off (rules/anypercent.md: replayable from the raw schedule; one key press per frame, so a verb key cannot share a frame with a skip key; use a verb click there).

### 6. Lever 5, dialog choice timing: no gain of its own beyond lever 1; its real role is as the luck knob. A choice counts on the frame its menu line appears, so it is covered by the control-2..control+1 candidates.

- **Evidence:** full5's path has at least 334 dialog-line clicks in 192 stops (verb slots 120-128 in the stops' how names, /var/tmp/fast-mi/e031-research/chain.json). docs/anypercent/findings.md: menus draw one random number per frame, so delaying a choice by d frames shifts every later draw; the hand driver steered the insult fights this way (play_steered, 1-60 frames).
- **Why it matters:** Choose the earliest frame unless the group is draw-marked; then the choice frame is where lever 7's delay scan acts, because the RNG is known to move per frame there.

### 7. Lever 6a, walking: the exit's nearest pixel (and any click whose walk target is the clicked pixel). Procedure: for each click that leads to a room change, sample other pixels with the same hit (the engine's hit test gives the object per pixel; border pixels on an 8-px grid, up to 40); fork, click, run to the room change; keep the earliest. Stop after 3 samples if arrival is identical (a fixed walk point). Local test: room-change frame plus the next group's completion, because the entry point in the next room moves with the pixel.

- **Evidence:** Engine measurement made for this report (high9 seq at full5 stop leg02:18751, a 132-frame map crossing): the route's own pixel (79,195) gives the room change at +132; the object's nearest pixel (103,192) gives +125, 7 frames sooner (5.3%), and ego lands in the next room at (316,16) instead of (305,19). Outputs: /var/tmp/fast-mi/e031-research/execopt2/seq0.out and seq2.out. The search clicks near an object's centre.
- **Why it matters:** Cheap: 0.2-0.6 s per exit, 2-5 minutes for the route's 464 room entries. Gain is a guess: 300-900 frames (2,000-5,000 ticks), since only exits with pixel-defined targets gain and their count is unknown until the scan runs.

### 8. Lever 6b, walking: re-clicks during a walk (a new target resets the step fractions; the first step rounds up moving left or up, down moving right or down). Procedure: on every player walk, a per-frame search over click or no click, with floor points a little ahead of ego as targets and the destination's own click last; first the direction rule alone, a beam only where the rule gains. A re-click counts only if the engine's new-leg bit shows the walk was re-planned.

- **Evidence:** E025's probes on the Fate of Atlantis demo: 549 re-click frames, none costing a frame; 408 ticks route-wide with the direction rule and 492 with a width-6 beam, out of 6,804 player-walk ticks (6.0% and 7.2%); 23 s for 22 walks. The hand driver's _reclick_greedy and _reclick_sweep are the same idea but were switched on by hand on 11 and 2 moves. On full5: 1,074 player walks, 31,705 frames (median 22). My estimate from full5's own per-frame steps, left and up legs only: 887 frames (2.8%). 47.4% of its walking frames are in boxes at scale 255, where the step is already whole and the gain is about zero; 46% are in scaled boxes.
- **Why it matters:** Gain 900-1,900 frames (5,000-11,000 ticks, 1.5-3 min); the upper figure is E025's rate applied as is, so it is a scale, not a measurement on this game. Cost about 1 s per walk, 18 minutes for all walks on one engine.

### 9. Lever 6c, walking: waypoints, corridors, gate corners, diagonal slopes. The diagonal speed-up is real in this engine but its reach is much smaller than the engine-mechanics note suggests, and two of that note's map figures do not apply.

- **Evidence:** Engine measurement (seq1.out, same stop): on a slope just under the clamp ratio ego's x advanced 7 px in 6 frames against a cap of 1 px a frame, so the truncated clamp works in v5. But a floor click on that map switched the walk to the slow speed (about 1 px a frame against 4 for the object click): the crossing took 165 frames instead of 132. Box geometry (build/blocks-dos-cd, read for this analysis only): all 46 walk boxes of room 85 have zero area, so no zig-zag is possible there. Over full5's walking frames: 29.8% in line boxes, 20.7% in boxes thinner than 8 px, 28.9% in 8-24 px bands, 14.0% in wide boxes. E025's probes: gate corner 36->28 frames, zig-zag 19->16, two walks -138 jiffies verified from boot; full search capped at 600 arrival evaluations, median 40 s or less per walk.
- **Why it matters:** Zig-zag can act on at most about 43% of walking frames, and the system must let the engine judge every waypoint (a waypoint can change the walk speed). Gain guess 1,000-1,600 frames (3-5% of walking). Cost 11-40 s per walk, so only for walks of 20 frames or more that pass a 5 s screen: about 300 walks, 15-40 minutes on 5 workers. Lowest gain per compute of the levers that gain anything.

### 10. Lever 6d, walking: scroll and approach. The search never clicks during a walk, so a walk to something off screen is a chain of walks that each run to their end. Procedure (E025's FirstPoint and approach click): keep walking toward the screen edge on the destination's side and click the destination on the first frame it has a point on screen.

- **Evidence:** 1,988 of 1,994 clicks come from an idle frame. full5 has 52 multi-click walks: 251 clicks, 12,520 frames. A click during a walk costs no frame (E025: actor.cpp:918). The hand route needed approach_hold on 4 moves because renewing the target on every camera step floors the step on axes moving right or down.
- **Why it matters:** Gain is a guess, 300-1,000 frames, on top of lever 1 (which already removes the idle between the chained walks). It is a small extension of the re-click search: one more candidate per frame (the destination, when it has a point).

### 11. Lever 7, luck by delay at random draws. Procedure: mark draw-dependent groups with three twin forks whose RNG is perturbed (compare every global, bit, owner, state and class, and the completion frame). In the forward pass, at each marked group try delays 0..12 frames (more on failure) at the last input before the draw, where the RNG moves per frame; judge by the completion of the group that consumes the draw (found by the twins: the first group whose completion differs); keep the earliest. This must be the last decision taken at each point of one forward pass.

- **Evidence:** Hand run (docs/anypercent/overnight.md, notes/log/2026-10-21-history-to-date.md): catacomb exits 59 s -> 12 s with 0-12 frames of delay and one goal of look-ahead (-47 s); fights and one other dialog -5.4 s; about 52 s in all (3.5% of the run). Look-ahead before every slow goal cost 24 s, because one random stream runs from boot and any earlier delay reshuffles later draws; a merge left the stream 317 draws off with no exact re-sync. The hand driver's general parts: delay_lookahead and play_steered. Its hand parts: a name pattern for which goals to scan, and a game variable that names the right exit. full5: the catacomb room takes 2,201 frames (13,200 ticks) over 21 visits; the store's combination is drawn at frame 3,081 and used at 20,735.
- **Why it matters:** Gain guess on full5: 5,000-25,000 ticks. Cost: 13 delays times a two-group look-ahead, about 0.4 s per decision; about 4 s when the draw is consumed thousands of frames later. Limit: a delay only re-times fixed inputs. Where the draw decides which inputs are needed (which exit, how many repeats), the consuming group must be found again, by the frozen search in a short corridor or by ddmin over repeated sentences. That is route work, not timing.

### 12. How a local gain is accepted when a draw follows: three levels, cheapest first. (1) Exact rejoin: the bridge's state digest (RNG included) equals the baseline's at a later control return; the rest of the route is then the same game shifted earlier and needs no look-ahead. (2) State equal but RNG different: try giving back 0..gain frames of delay to hit the baseline's RNG; if that fails, accept only if the route replays through the next draw-marked group with the same outcome. (3) After the last draw-marked group, local gains carry one for one.

- **Evidence:** E025: after the last draw 138 jiffies = 54 + 84, exact; before a draw 1 local gain in 4 survived (two walks gained 6 frames each and cost 1,002 frames at a draw-dependent step). The hand run: letting local search beat every hint was slower, twice. The Status reply carries the RNG value and the digest covers the whole save state plus RNG.
- **Why it matters:** Without this, walk and buffering gains before a draw are a lottery and can cost more than they save. With it, the order of work follows: timing levers in one forward pass, the luck decision last at each draw, and walking search first in the stretch after the last draw, where it is safe and cheapest to verify.

### 13. Which parts of the hand route's driver are general and which rest on hand hints.

- **Evidence:** scripts/anypercent_auto.py and src/speedrun/executor.py. General: click at gate+1; early shifts of 2 and 1 frames with a pure-shift test; greedy per-frame re-clicks judged by finish frame on forks (floor points 16 and 40 px ahead, 8 px to each side, two tie orders); the every-frame sweep; delay look-ahead judged at the next goal; retry with 1-60 frames of delay. Hand hints: each move's until and after conditions (418 of 532 moves have after), which moves re-click (11), sweep (2), hand-stepped walk phases (7), approach hold (4), no_early (9), fixed delays (6), the goal-name pattern for look-ahead, the exit read from a game variable, the timer variable numbers left out of the early signature, the slide counters.
- **Why it matters:** Every hand hint has an automatic stand-in the optimizer needs: until -> the baseline's state at the next control return; the timer variables -> keys measured to change by themselves on no-input forks; no_early and the name pattern -> twin-fork draw marks; the re-click flags -> a cheap screen on every walk. None of them needs a game number in the code.

### 14. Ranking by gain per compute, on full5 as it is (ticks saved per engine-second; gains other than lever 1's pool are estimates).

- **Evidence:** 1. Earliest input after control: about 290,000 ticks for 2-30 minutes (160 to 2,400 ticks/s). 2. Verb/object spacing: about 12,000-16,000 ticks for 20 s (inside lever 1's pool). 3. Buffering before control: about 2,000 ticks for about a minute. 4. Luck by delay: 5,000-25,000 ticks for 1-10 minutes, high variance. 5. Exit nearest pixel: 2,000-5,000 ticks for 2-5 minutes. 6. Re-clicks, direction rule: 5,000-11,000 ticks for 18 minutes. 7. Approach clicks: 2,000-6,000 ticks, cost about as re-clicks. 8. Waypoints, corridors, diagonals: 6,000-10,000 ticks for 1-3 engine-hours. 9. Dialog choice timing and per-line text skipping: 0 beyond lever 1. Unit costs: fork about 4 ms, about 19,000 frames/s, E024's 13.5 ms per judged candidate.
- **Why it matters:** Levers 1-3 are one pass and give about 45% of the route for minutes of compute. Everything in walking together is about 15,000-30,000 ticks (2-5% of the original route) for most of the compute. On a hand-quality route these same levers were worth about 70 s of 23 minutes, most of it luck. So the plan should build the forward re-timing pass first and spend the saved laptop hours on route structure, not on deep walk search.

## Open points

- How many of the 53,777 removable idle frames the game needs (waiting on a timer or a character). The 85-95% figure is a guess until the re-timing pass runs; the 11 idle runs over 200 frames (6,545 frames) and the search's 33 wait trials (8,550 frames) are where it is decided.
- In which rooms the RNG moves every frame. A delay can steer or restore a draw only there. The per-frame replay file has no RNG column; one replay recording Status rng at every control return would settle it.
- How many groups the twins mark as draw-dependent on full5, and how far away each draw is consumed. This sets the cost of the luck pass and how much of the walking search is safe.
- How many of the 464 room entries use a pixel-defined walk target. Only those gain from the nearest-pixel scan; I measured one.
- The diagonal clamp at normal walk speed in a wide box, in the engine. I confirmed it only at the slow map speed. The +12% figure in docs/research/anypercent-engine-mechanics.md is still a mirror result.
- Whether dropping or moving the 1,921 pointer-parking moves changes any later state (the hover script reads the pointer). Lever 2 needs them gone between consecutive clicks.
- E025's fork label clash (a fork_each child showing another room than a fresh fork at the same frame) has no known cause. Every lever here judges on forks, so each kept result needs a plain replay check until it is found.
- E025's walk stage was never built; its rates come from probes on another game. The first walk milestone should re-measure the direction rule on 20 of full5's walks before any full walk search is budgeted.
- Under the logo glitch (var 19 = 5), waits counted in jiffies take more frames, so inner offsets inside a group can break. The boot decision has to come before this pass, or the pass has to be rerun after it.
- Real time against ticks in the standalone launch on this game is unmeasured; if fades add time there, the nearest-pixel and walk judges must score in quarter-frames, not ticks.
- Another agent's engine was running during my three seq runs (load 30). My runs were about 12 s each; the outputs are deterministic, but the wall times are not a clean cost measure.
