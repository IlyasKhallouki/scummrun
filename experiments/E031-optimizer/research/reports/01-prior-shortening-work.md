# The project's own shortening and optimisation work

A research report made for E031's plan (2026-11-16) by a reading agent; its claims were used in the plan after the plan's critics checked them. Evidence paths are as the agent gave them.

## Findings

### 1. E024's forward pass is the only shortening method the project has run end to end. A movie from boot is cut into steps (a step ends wherever progress changes at a control stop: room, objects ego owns, the guide's held atoms). Each step is then found again from boot in order, with skips on from frame 1. Every frame t from the previous step's stop is tried, the step's inputs are re-placed by what they act on (anchors), and the earliest completion is kept. Clicks are never shifted.

- **Evidence:** /home/ilyask/projects/scummrun/experiments/E024-shorten/plan.md Part 2 items 1-9 and amendment 3; code in /home/ilyask/projects/scummrun/experiments/E011-speed/rs/fmi-shorten/src (chain.rs, label.rs, scan.rs, pass.rs, refind.rs, verdict.rs, movie.rs, skips.rs). Seed 1 (~/.cache/fast-mi-e024/run2-seed1/run-001.json): the input movie was 97 inputs, 30,785 frames, 182,260 ticks (50:38). The forward pass reached frame 2,671, tick 14,152, in 78.7 s on 8 workers. 34 steps were found by the scan, 1 by the relaxed goal, 1 with skips off (the voyage) and 1 by an offer trial (the cave exit). Labelling took 3.6 s. Each version was verified from boot by two launches (33 of 33 digests), and the reviewer's rerun was byte-identical.
- **Why it matters:** This is a ready mechanism for full5 that leaves the search alone. Re-timing every step from the previous completion is what removed E024's four waits of about 7,200 frames each, and the skips-from-boot start is part of the same pass. Most of the 182k → 14k drop came from those waits and from skips, not from cleverness.

### 2. E024's reductions are drops: one step, a room loop (steps that leave a room and come back), ddmin chunks (halves, quarters, eighths) and single inner inputs. They are tried in order of measured waste (the ticks each step adds). Each test first runs a cheap anchored replay, and a full re-find of the rest of the route only if that replay reaches the goal. A drop is kept only for an earlier end verified from boot. The gain was small, and the cost grows with the square of the step count.

- **Evidence:** reduce.rs; run-001.json: round 1 tested 110 drops in 88 s, and the cheap replay rejected 106 of them in about 0.1 s each. Three were kept: step 34, one input of step 33, one of step 21. That gave −546 ticks (14,152 → 13,606, 3:46.8; 92 clicks). Round 2 gained 0. The whole stage took 282 s. E024 G6: 'reduction rounds grow with the square of the step count, about 3.3 h a round for Part I' (Part I is about 10× the demo's steps). Known faults: a later pass does not rerun the fallback that found a step (F1). Step 25 was re-found 288 frames late (2,439 against 2,151), so E024's step-15 drop was wrongly judged 'not earlier' (E025 plan, Problem).
- **Why it matters:** Run as built on full5 (about 670 steps, see the scale finding), the same square law gives roughly 10 h per round. Each test also re-boots the prefix (about 3 s) and can trigger a full re-find of the rest. That breaks the few-hours limit. Reductions need a horizon (re-find a few steps after the drop, then check that the state rejoins the old route) and a cap on candidates by waste before they can run on full5.

### 3. Skips from boot (skips.rs) is the cheapest measured saving for full5. full5 plays the intro without skips: its skips event at frame 1 only takes effect at the bridge's segment start, the first control.

- **Evidence:** In full5's chain the first stop is at frame 2,728 and the first click at 2,729. The same boot is frame 2728 / tick 16,321 / qf 65,284 in /var/tmp/fast-mi/e012-archives/kitchen/stage.json root. E024's G7 on that Monkey Island chain (commit 054378d) measured skips from frame 1 passing the var-19 rule, with first control at frame 110, tick 660, var 19 = 6. host.rs:121 already passes SPEEDRUN_START_FRAME and SPEEDRUN_TIMER_NEXT to the engine.
- **Why it matters:** This is about −15,661 ticks (−4:21, 2.3% of 694,701) before any other change. But it moves every later frame by about 2,618, so the whole route has to be found again (E024: 'skips switched on before frame 18741 broke the fixed clicks'). It cannot be a one-line edit of the movie.

### 4. For Any%, skips.rs's var-19 rule is the opposite of what we want. It measures var 19 at first control (6) and requires it. That bans the logo speed glitch, which Any% allows.

- **Evidence:** skips.rs docstring and setup(). /home/ilyask/projects/scummrun/docs/anypercent/findings.md 'DOS CD': an Esc on frames 46 to 92 from boot leaves var 19 at 5. /home/ilyask/projects/scummrun/rules/anypercent.md allows the logo glitch, pixel input, re-clicks during walks, walkbox exploits, saves made during the run, and a chosen seed. /home/ilyask/projects/scummrun/docs/comparison.md §1.4: with every frame at 5 ticks instead of 6, the upper bound on Part I was −62 s out of 373 s. Delays counted in jiffies keep their length.
- **Why it matters:** The frame of the first Esc is a one-number search the system can judge by var 19 and the verified end. A route under var 19 = 5 has different frame timing throughout (jiffy waits take more frames), so it again needs a full re-find. Capped at 1/6 of the frame-paced ticks, it is the largest single Any% lever.

### 5. full5's own route shows where a shortener could gain. 66% of its frames lead to stops with no news, a fifth of the route sits inside room loops with no news, and the search's explicit waits add 8,550 frames.

- **Evidence:** A python walk of /var/tmp/fast-mi/direct/full5/leg00..leg03/nodes.jsonl from top stops 15923/11189/22320/7895. The concatenated own events equal end-stop.jsonl (3,916 events). Counts: 1,038 stops on the chain, 266 with news; 772 without news cover 76,168 frames. 464 room changes over 82 distinct rooms. 33 wait stops (15 of 50, 11 of 200, 7 of 800 frames, 8,550 wait frames; 22 with no news). 52 'go' stops cover 12,520 frames. 84 returns to a room with no news since the last visit; the union of those spans is 22,100 frames (19%). 36 click gaps over 200 frames total 14,081 frames. 6,399 frames run from the last click (108,579) to the end (114,978).
- **Why it matters:** These are upper bounds by the search's own news measure (a loop with no news can still be needed: a timer, a script), but they rank the work. First the boot. Then the waits, which the scan re-times for free. Then loop drops by waste, through reduce.rs's 'loop' candidate. The end stretch after the last click cannot be cut by drops.

### 6. E025 (walking) was planned and never built. The branch e025-walks has no commits past the plan, and only a work-in-progress patch for its Part 1 (the objective becomes real time, qf) exists. Its probes measured what walk changes are worth and how random draws decide whether a local gain survives.

- **Evidence:** /home/ilyask/projects/scummrun/experiments/E025-walking/plan.md (93332f6); `git log main..e025-walks` is empty; ~/.cache/fast-mi-e025/paused/e025-stageA-qf-wip.patch; MEMORY: E025 paused on 11-10. Probes: walking was 56.9% of route-004's ticks. Re-clicks: 549 re-click frames, none costing a frame; the `dir` rule gives about 408 ticks route-wide, a width-6 beam 492. Room 48, walks 29 + 33: −138 jiffies at the end, verified from boot by both launches. Gate corners 36→28 frames, a zig-zag 19→16, a split leg −1. Before the last draw a local gain survived 1 time in 4 (walk 12 kept; walks 2 and 6 gained 6 frames locally but cost +1,002 frames at the quiz). After the last draw local gains carried one for one (138 = 54 + 84). Prediction: −276 jiffies (−2%); about 45 min per walk round.
- **Why it matters:** Re-clicking during walks ('shmoovement') and waypoint clicks are banned in glitchless but allowed in Any%, so E025's proposals become legal moves for full5. Its local-acceptance-to-next-draw design is also the cheaper acceptance test the reductions need. Expect small gains (about 2%), and real losses wherever a draw follows.

### 7. The tick count understates real time. Any% is timed in real time, so the objective must be qf (quarter-frames), which only the paused E025 patch introduces.

- **Evidence:** E024 amendment 3 and E025's realtime probe: route-004 is 13,606 ticks but 13,888.75 jiffies (3:51.5). The 282.75 missing jiffies are 29 screen fades of 39 qf each, all at room changes or in the boot. rules/anypercent.md: 'The clock must also count every wall-clock wait outside the main loop (fades, transitions)'. scan.rs's Found key is (tick, frame, t), with no qf on main.
- **Why it matters:** full5 has 464 room changes. If Monkey Island's fades cost the same 39 qf (not measured on this game), that is about 4,500 jiffies (about 75 s) of real time missing from its 694,701 ticks. A loop drop then gains its fades as well, and a judge that counts ticks would rank candidates wrongly.

### 8. Exactly what runs on full5 with the current fmi binary: the diagnostics. One data conversion is needed first (end-stop.jsonl holds its events as one array; read_movie wants one event per line). The binary's Monkey Island build and engine must match full5's (svm-e027 with 0013, E027's env.sh).

- **Evidence:** fmi/src/shorten.rs: read_movie parses lines; event_of accepts the search's click, move and skips events. `fmi shorten trace --movie M --seed 1 --to 114979 OUT` prints frame, tick, room, xstart, cam, idle, ego, mv and v19 per frame, and needs no archive. `fmi shorten verify --ends` takes its engine settings only from a step-log first line ({"meta":{"env":[...]}}). trace's --env is split on commas, so it cannot carry SPEEDRUN_GOAL='[{"bit":565,"in":[1]}]'. A one-line stub step log can. fmi-direct depends only on fmi-core, fmi-engine and fmi-explore (fmi-direct/Cargo.toml), never on fmi-shorten.
- **Why it matters:** trace gives the per-stretch time breakdown (idle frames before each click, time per room) to rank the work, with no code change and no effect on the gate.

### 9. With data adapters only (no code change), `fmi shorten label` and `fmi shorten run --rounds 0` can run on full5 through a stand-in archive folder. A small code change in fmi/fmi-shorten would make this clean.

- **Evidence:** Held::load (chain.rs:63-82) reads only stage.json (guide path, goal keys, e009.idle_frames, seed), log.jsonl (idle keys, only when idle_frames ≠ 0) and entries/0.json.gz (the last checkpoint's facts as the root). goal_env turns stage.json's goal into SPEEDRUN_GOAL ('bit:565':[1] gives {"bit":565,"in":[1]}). /var/tmp/fast-mi/e012-archives/kitchen/entries/0.json.gz holds the root facts at frame 2728, seed 1: the same boot as full5. guide-end.json (the e008-guide-1 format, 1,161 atoms, 805 keys) loads through guide_atoms. Command: `fmi shorten run --movie full5.jsonl --archive STANDIN --goal facts --seed 1 --movie-seed 1 --skips --tail 7000 --workers 8 --rounds 0 OUT`. --tail must cover the 6,399 frames after the last click (the default is 3000). Otherwise the label replay sees no goal and run stops with 'the movie does not end the game'.
- **Why it matters:** This gives the first measured answers on full5 within the rules: the step count, anchors and raw inputs, draw-marked steps (twins), G2's re-placement check, and a verified forward pass under skips from boot. The cleaner version is a --guide/--goal-facts input path in fmi/src/shorten.rs (Held::new is public). Both stay outside the search's code.

### 10. Inputs the direct search's movie lacks or carries differently: no idle keys, 1,921 pointer moves that would be dropped, and a goal that is a flag, not the game's end.

- **Evidence:** Idle keys: E024 took them from the explorer archive's log (keys that change by themselves). The direct search has no such log; the kitchen archive's cover only its own stage. Moves: the input log records only keys and clicks (fmi-core lib.rs:1460), and pass::specs places only inputs with a Taken record (pass.rs:85-94), so full5's 1,921 'move' events (pointer parked at (0,199) or (319,199) after each click) are not placed in a re-found route. Goal: full5 ended on --until bit:565=1 (verify-end.json keys bit:565), not on a restart or quit. Goal::End needs the game's end with its site, so --goal facts is required.
- **Why it matters:** Without idle keys, a key that changes on its own falls into progress. That cuts spurious steps, and keep(k) then demands a timer value at another time, so steps fail. A few lines of no-input forks at control stops can measure idle keys (chain.rs's `probe(e, None)` already does this for the interface's keys). Whether dropping the pointer moves changes anything is unmeasured; G2 on full5 would show it.

### 11. There is a measured predictor that the forward pass as built stops early on a Monkey Island route. E024's G7 ran it on E012's kitchen chain from the same boot, under skips, and it stopped at step 5 of 12. The two walk failure classes that stopped E024 are E025's fixes, which are designed and not built.

- **Evidence:** E024 amendment 3 G7: 'the pass stops at step 5 of 12, where clicks made while ego walks have no point at 1,680 of 1,761 t's'. Realize, 260 offer trials, the dialog search and skips off all completed nothing. E025 stuck probe: 1,532 of those t's come from the key `o` placed too early, 148 from walk anchors (camera lag; actors standing on the point), and step 4 completed at 335, before step 3's 341 (F2). G4 (no skips) stopped at the cave exit, an exit off screen until ego walks (E025 Part 5, approach trials). F3: a walk anchor uses the stop's xstart, while the game reads the click after that frame's moveCamera; box_of takes the lowest box, the engine the highest. F4: a fork_each child labelled 1407 showed another room than the base fork at 1407 (cause not found).
- **Why it matters:** full5's opening already walks between room 33 and room 30 with repeated target clicks (stops 'w6', 'go34', 't387.t387.t387'). Before a long run, E025's F2, F3, the Lead(n)/FirstPoint timing and approach clicks should be built and gated on the kitchen (the project's fix-known-gaps-first rule). F4 needs its cause found first, since the scan relies on fork_each.

### 12. Random draws are the main desync risk. Shifting clicks is never done. Each input is re-placed by anchor and judged by the input log. Draw keys are found by three twin forks with a perturbed RNG: each step is judged strictly by value first and relaxed ('changed from the root') second. A draw that comes out differently can still block a step.

- **Evidence:** E024 Problem: room 82's cave getRandomNr(6), the quiz's Var[239]/[240], actors on random walks and the scroll position all change when clicks move. Amendment 1: three twins, because one twin misses a 6-way draw 1 time in 6 (three: 1 in 216). Seed 3 stopped at the quiz's last line: its draw differed, and realize, 31 offer trials, the dialog search (43 leaves) and skips off found nothing. Draw-aware judging is out of scope in E025. The dialog search is capped at 2,000 leaves (refind.rs LEAVES). Other pitfalls: var:56, the sound result, differs on forks (FMI_SKIP_SOUNDRESULT; `fmi check verify-new` does not skip it). FMI_MEM_FILES (patch 0010) is required, or concurrent forks share the save folder's file (one read a torn file). Completion is not monotone in t, so there is no bisection. The earliest completion per step is greedy, not a global optimum.
- **Why it matters:** Skips from boot shifts the whole route, so every random event in full5's route is drawn again. Which steps the twins mark is unknown until a label run. Steps whose dialog lines answer a drawn value are where a full5 forward pass is most likely to need the dialog search or to stop.

### 13. Scale projection for full5, from E024's measured costs: labelling and the forward pass fit in a few hours; the reductions as built do not.

- **Evidence:** Measured unit costs (run-001.json): an accepted candidate costs 13.5 ms + 0.034 ms per frame; a refused one 2.6 ms on average. The demo ran 5.5 candidates per route frame (14,664 candidates over 2,671 frames; failed strict scans and fallbacks make up the excess). Replay speed: verify-end replayed full5's 114,978 frames from boot in 2.89 s. full5 has 669 stops where the room changes or news arrives (median span 106 frames, largest 6,425). The spans sum to 112,250 frames, and the sum of L²/2 is 4.1e7 candidate frames, which gives about 2,900 CPU s (0.1 h on 8 workers), or 0.56 h with the demo's 5.5× excess, plus dialog searches. Reductions: about 670 steps, 84 loops, 14 chunks and about 1,300 inner inputs, each test at least a 3 s re-boot of the prefix.
- **Why it matters:** These are projections, not measurements. G6-style counts from a real label run and a forward pass on full5 must replace them before any reduction round is planned (the project's performance-first rule).

### 14. docs/optimization.md and docs/comparison.md describe the older Python optimiser for glitchless Part I. Its action model is hand-written (named PDDL actions such as open-bar-door), so it cannot be reused under the no-hand-moves rule. Two parts are reusable: choosing by measurement, and a cost table keyed by where ego starts.

- **Evidence:** optimization.md: Fast Downward astar(lmcut) over the model with measured mean ticks. The keyed surrogate has 103 position tokens. 43 candidates (bootstrap and perturbed costs) were selected on seeds 1-30 and the winner reported on held-out seeds 31-60: 22,292 ticks against 23,988 for unit cost (−1,697, −7%), in 45 planner runs, 1,932 s. Keyed surrogate error −22 to +13 ticks; pooled −275 to +115. One walk's cost changes by hundreds of ticks with ego's entry point (dock→lookout 642 against 1,008). comparison.md: walking was 77% of Part I's ticks. The listed human tricks (logo glitch, re-clicks, early door entry, floor clicks) are banned in glitchless and allowed under Any%.
- **Why it matters:** Steps found per step and greedily cannot reorder the route. A reorder or choice layer over the system's own measured steps, keyed by context, gained 7% on the old Part I plan. That would be new work, and it needs the dependencies between steps. Any% lets the run name its seed, so the multi-seed selection is optional.

### 15. The search tree held stops that reached the same level and object count earlier than the stops each continuation leg started from. Most news keys on the end chain were also reached earlier on other branches.

- **Evidence:** Same python pass over nodes.jsonl. leg00 continued from frame 42,504 (level 20, 20 objects), but node 9232 reached level 20 with as many objects at 32,712 (9,792 frames earlier). leg01: 51,734 against 48,128. leg02: 92,860 against 90,098. 298 of the chain's 554 news keys were first reached earlier elsewhere in the same leg (largest lead 30,737 frames).
- **Why it matters:** This is E024's out-of-scope A4 ('fastest arrival per cell'). A shortener could try re-rooting a later leg's steps from an earlier equivalent stop, re-finding them by anchor and verifying from boot, without touching the search. Those stops differ in other state, so this is a measured lead, not a gain.

### 16. E024's video tool needs a verified route with its step log, which full5 does not have.

- **Evidence:** /home/ilyask/projects/scummrun/experiments/E024-shorten/video/make.sh runs `fmi shorten verify` with SPEEDRUN_VIDEO in the standalone launch, and render.py draws the clicks, skip keys and a real-time timer (jiffies / 59.998). verify needs ROUTE.steps.jsonl (movie.rs: 'a movie without its step log fails').
- **Why it matters:** For a video of full5 or of a shortened route: a route written by `fmi shorten run` works directly. For full5 as it is, the tool would need the `--ends` path plus a stub step log whose meta.env holds SPEEDRUN_GOAL.
